A design that looks right on screen can still be the wrong size in real life.
  1. A design's size relative to its real, actual-size object or space is called , usually written as a such as 1:2 or 1:10.
  2. If a scale is 1:10, one unit on the drawing equals real units. If a real box is 20 inches wide and you're drawing it at a scale of 1:4, your drawing should be inches wide.
  3. Keeping the size relationship between different parts of a design consistent when you scale it up or down is called . Scaling only the width and not the height distorts this relationship.
  4. A ruler with several sets of graduated markings, one per scale ratio, used to measure or draw at scale without doing the math for every measurement, is a .
