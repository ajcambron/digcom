### 1. Prepare
* **The Draft**: Ensure your work is exported or ready to view. It doesn’t have to be perfect—placeholders for missing pieces are okay!
* **The Plus/Delta Chart**: Create a simple T-chart on a piece of paper or a digital document:
    * **Plus (+)**: What is working well? (Strengths, clear storytelling, strong technique).
    * **Delta (Δ)**: What should be changed or improved? (The Greek symbol for "change").

### 2. The Review Process
* **Silent First Pass**: Experience the work all the way through once without speaking. This allows the reviewer to experience the "flow" of the piece.
* **Second Pass**: Go through it again, but this time, the reviewer should jot down specific notes in the Plus and Delta columns.
* **The Golden Rule**: Feedback should be **Specific, Objective, and Kind**.
    * *Bad:* "I didn't like the ending."
    * *Good:* "The ending felt abrupt because the music cut off before the reporter finished speaking."

### 3. Giving Feedback (The Reviewer)
* **Identify the "Pluses"**: Start with what is successful.
* **Suggest the "Deltas"**: Instead of just pointing out a mistake, offer a solution.
    * *Example:* "The transition at 0:45 is a bit slow; maybe try a simple cut instead of a dissolve."

### 4. Receiving Feedback (The Creator)
* **Listen Without Defending**: It’s tempting to explain why you made a choice, but for now, just listen and take notes. If a peer is confused, a general audience likely will be too.
* **Ask Clarifying Questions**: If a note is vague, ask for the exact timestamp or location: "Can you show me exactly where the audio got too loud?"

### 5. The Revision Plan
After the session, look at your Delta column and prioritize your edits:
1.  **Level 1 (Critical)**: Technical errors (missing audio, black frames, typos).
2.  **Level 2 (Message)**: Pacing issues or confusing sequences.
3.  **Level 3 (Polishing)**: Color tweaks, fancy transitions, or extra effects.
