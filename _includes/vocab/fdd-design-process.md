**Idea**
: A thought or suggestion as to a possible course of action; a concept or mental impression; the
aim or purpose.

**Abstract**
: Existing in thought or as an idea but not having a physical or concrete existence — the
opposite of representational.

**Representational**
: Relating to or characterized by depiction of the physical appearance of things.

**Ambiguity**
: Uncertainness of meaning — in design, this allows an image to be read more than one way.

**Format**
: The layout of a design — the surface area a composition is created in and its boundaries,
such as the size of a business card or brochure.

**Composition**
: A complete work of art or design, seen in total, not as individual visual elements.

**Thumbnail Sketch**
: A small, quick sketch — a fast way to generate many ideas.

**Rough Sketch**
: A mid-stage development of a graphic idea, more developed than a thumbnail, often shown to a
client for approval.

**Comprehensive Design ("Comp")**
: The final, neatly executed rendering of a graphic idea — short for "comprehensive."

**Ubiquitous**
: Present, appearing, or found everywhere.

**Line**
: An identifiable path created by a point moving in space. One-dimensional; can vary in width,
direction, and length. Lines often define the edges of a shape, and can be horizontal, vertical,
or diagonal, straight or curved, thick or thin.

**Shape**
: The form of an object or its external boundary, outline, or external surface, as opposed to
other properties such as color or texture.

**Texture**
: An interwoven pattern of light and dark tones that imparts a physical quality to a
surface — rough, smooth, etc.
