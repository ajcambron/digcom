**Close-Up (CU)**
: A shot that fills the frame with a subject's face (roughly chin to forehead) or a small object,
emphasizing detail and emotion.

**Extreme Close-Up (ECU)**
: A shot even tighter than a close-up, isolating one specific detail — an eye, a hand, a ticking
watch — for maximum emphasis.

**Medium Close-Up (MCU)**
: A shot framed from roughly the chest up — closer than a medium shot but not as tight as a
close-up; the standard framing for most interviews.

**Medium Shot (MS)**
: A shot framed from roughly the waist up, showing enough body language to read gesture while
keeping the face clearly visible.

**Wide Shot (WS)**
: A shot that shows a subject's full body along with a good amount of their surrounding
environment.

**Long Shot (LS)**
: A shot showing a subject at a distance, small within the frame, emphasizing their relationship
to a larger environment.

**Extreme Long Shot (ELS)**
: A very wide shot, often used to establish a location, where any subject is tiny compared to the
surrounding environment.

**Over-the-Shoulder (OTS)**
: A shot framed from behind one person's shoulder, looking toward another person or subject —
common in dialogue scenes, showing both perspective and connection between two people.

**Point of View (POV)**
: A shot that shows exactly what a character sees, placing the camera at their eye line so the
audience shares their perspective directly.

**Cutaway**
: A brief shot of something other than the main action — a reaction, a detail, a related image —
inserted to add context, hide an edit, or control pacing.
