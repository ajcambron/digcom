**Line**
: An element of art defined by a point moving in space. May be two- or three-dimensional,
descriptive, implied, or abstract.

**Shape**
: An element of art that is two-dimensional, flat, or limited to height and width.

**Color / Value**
: Color is the perceived hue of an object; value is the lightness or darkness of a tone or color
(white lightest, black darkest, middle gray halfway between).

**Texture**
: An element of art that refers to the way things feel, or look as if they might feel if
touched.

**Mass/Form**
: The way a three-dimensional form (or the illusion of one) appears to have weight, density, or
bulk, based on how it's created.

**Balance**
: The distribution of one or more elements so they visually equal each other. Unequal
distribution reads as asymmetrical.

**Alignment**
: The lining up of text or graphics on a page.

**Contrast**
: The range of light to dark areas in a composition — greater contrast means a greater
distinction between light and dark regions.

**Unity/Harmony**
: Design elements working together both visually and conceptually, with no single element
overpowering the whole.

**Proportion**
: The harmonious relation of parts to each other or to the whole.

**Proximity**
: Elements placed near each other read as related; elements spaced apart read as separate groups.

**Repetition**
: Repeating a single element multiple times across a design.

**Rule of Thirds**
: A layout principle suggesting a composition is most pleasing when key objects sit at the
intersections of lines dividing the image into thirds — used to draw emphasis to important
elements.

**Rule of Phi / Golden Ratio**
: A layout principle based on the mathematical ratio phi, historically used in art and
architecture to create naturally pleasing proportions (related to the Fibonacci spiral).
