**Typeface vs. Font**
: A typeface is a family of related letterform designs (e.g., Helvetica); a font is one specific
weight, style, and size within that family (e.g., Helvetica Bold, 12 pt). In everyday speech the
two words are often used interchangeably.

**Serif**
: A small line or stroke finishing off the end of a letter's main strokes.

**Sans-Serif**
: A typeface without serifs — generally cleaner, more geometric letterforms.

**Script / Display**
: Script typefaces imitate handwriting or calligraphy; display typefaces are drawn for large,
attention-getting use (headlines, logos, posters) rather than long passages of body text.

**Baseline**
: The invisible line that most letters visually "sit" on.

**X-Height**
: The height of a lowercase "x" in a given typeface — a rough measure of how large its lowercase
letters appear at a given point size.

**Cap Height**
: The height of a capital letter, measured up from the baseline.

**Ascender / Descender**
: The part of a lowercase letter that extends above the x-height (an ascender, as in "b" or "h"),
or below the baseline (a descender, as in "p" or "g").

**Counter**
: The fully or partially enclosed space within a letterform — for example, the hole inside an
"o" or "e."
