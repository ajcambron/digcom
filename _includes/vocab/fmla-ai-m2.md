**Additive Color**
: Color made by adding light. Red, green and blue light added together make white (RGB).

**Branding Style Guide**
: A document that sets the rules for how a brand looks: logos, colors, fonts and design elements.

**Client Brief**
: A document that explains a project's goals, target audience and deliverables.

**CMYK**
: Cyan, magenta, yellow and black (key): the four ink colors used in printing.

**Color Gamut**
: The range of colors a device or color mode can reproduce. A color outside it is out of gamut.

**HEX**
: A six-character code (#RRGGBB) that names a color for the web.

**HSB**
: Hue, saturation and brightness: a way to describe a color by its pigment, intensity and lightness.

**Logo**
: A symbol or design an organization uses to identify itself.

**Non-Objective Visual Elements**
: Shapes, lines, colors and textures that don't picture anything real. They keep a brand consistent and attractive.

**RGB**
: Red, green and blue: the colors of light a screen uses to display images.

**Sans Serif**
: A typeface without the small strokes (serifs) at the ends of letters. Common on screens, signs and logos.

**Scope Creep**
: When a project grows beyond what was planned and agreed to.

**Serif**
: A typeface with small strokes at the ends of letters. Common in printed body text.

**Subtractive Color**
: Color made by ink absorbing light. Cyan, magenta and yellow inks combine toward black (CMYK).

**Visual Hierarchy**
: Arranging elements so the viewer's eye moves through them in order of importance.

**Wordmark**
: A logo made only of text, like "Dobson National Park."
