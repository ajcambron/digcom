**Pixel**
: The smallest single point of color information in a digital image or video frame — short for
"picture element."

**Frame Rate**
: The number of individual still frames played per second to create the illusion of motion,
expressed in frames per second (fps) — e.g., 24fps for film, 30fps for standard video.

**Frame Size**
: The width and height of a video frame, measured in pixels (e.g., 1920×1080 for standard HD).

**Aspect Ratio**
: The proportional relationship between a video frame's width and height, expressed as a ratio —
16:9 (widescreen), 9:16 (vertical/mobile), or 1:1 (square) are the most common today.

**Safe Area(s)**
: The portion of a video frame guaranteed to display correctly on every screen — action safe
(where important action should stay) and title safe (a smaller inner zone for text) — protecting
against cropping on some displays.

**Codec**
: Short for "coder/decoder" — the software that compresses raw video into a smaller file for
storage and playback, then decompresses it again to display; H.264 is the most widely
compatible codec.

**Rendering**
: The process a computer performs to combine a project's clips, effects, and audio into a
single finished, playable video file.
