**Dieline**
: A technical line drawing showing where a package's material will be cut, scored, and folded —
the structural blueprint a graphic design is placed onto before a package can be built.

**Structural Packaging Design**
: The 3D engineering of a package (its shape, folds, and how it opens/closes), as distinct from
the 2D graphic design applied to its surfaces.

**Packaging Mockup**
: A realistic preview — physical or digital — showing artwork as it will actually look wrapped
around or printed on a real, dimensional package, used to catch problems flat art can hide.

**Brand Identity**
: The consistent visual system (logo, color palette, typography, imagery style) that makes a
company or product instantly recognizable across every piece it produces.

**Brand Guidelines (Style Guide)**
: A reference document defining exactly how a brand's logo, colors, fonts, and imagery may be
used, so every designer who touches the brand produces consistent work.

**Promotional Collateral**
: The set of supporting marketing materials (flyers, posters, table tents, social ads, etc.)
built to promote a product, event, or brand alongside its primary packaging.

**Tagline**
: A short, memorable phrase used consistently alongside a brand's name to reinforce its identity
or value proposition.

**Call to Action (CTA)**
: The specific instruction a piece of promotional design gives its audience ("Visit us today,"
"Scan to order") telling them exactly what to do next.

**Product Label**
: The printed graphic applied directly to a product or its packaging, typically carrying
required information (name, contents, branding) as well as promotional design.
