# 11.1 | Elements of Motion

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-after-effects-basics.md %}

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-keyframes.md %}

Premiere edits existing footage. After Effects builds motion from nothing, a shape, a keyframe,
and a decision about how that shape moves between two points.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-11-1" title="11.1 Guided Notes: Elements of Motion" presentation="/assets/presentations/add11-1.html" %}

A **composition** is After Effects' workspace, layers stacked inside it, animated over a
timeline. Set a **keyframe** to lock a property's value (position, opacity, scale) at a specific
point in time. two or more keyframes and AE animates smoothly between them. Left at the default,
that motion is linear, constant speed, abrupt starts and stops. **Ease in** and **ease out**
gradually speed up or slow down the motion instead, the difference between something that looks
animated and something that looks alive.

> "Nothing in the real world starts or stops instantly. A ball doesn't hit top speed the moment
> you throw it. Linear keyframes ignore that; eased ones don't."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What does a keyframe actually lock in place?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does linear motion read as robotic compared to eased motion?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "You want a shape to feel like it's falling under gravity, speeding up
> as it drops. Which easing would you use, and why?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Create a new composition** and add a shape layer.
2. **Set position keyframes** moving the shape across the frame.
3. **Apply Ease In/Ease Out** to those keyframes and compare the motion to the un-eased version.
4. **Add opacity and scale keyframes** so the shape fades and grows as it moves.
5. **Turn and Talk:** compare your eased animation with a partner's before finalizing.

{: .discussion }
What changed visually between your linear version and your eased version of the same animation?

## Studio/Project Work Time (~30 min)

Extend your animation: add a second layer that reacts to or complements the first (for example, a
second shape that enters just as the first one settles). Keyframe its position, opacity, and
scale with eased interpolation, matching today's technique.

## Exit Ticket / Wrap-Up (~5 min)

Save your file, then screenshot the Timeline panel showing your keyframes.
