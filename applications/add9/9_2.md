# 9.2 | Auto Reframe & Multi-Platform Versions

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-multiplatform.md %}

The same video rarely gets delivered just once. A client wants a 16:9 cut for YouTube, a 9:16
cut for Stories, and a 1:1 cut for a feed post, and reframing each one by hand would take all day.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-9-2" title="9.2 Guided Notes: Auto Reframe" presentation="/assets/presentations/add9-2.html" %}

**Auto Reframe** uses **motion tracking** to detect a sequence's subject and automatically
reposition the frame as you convert to a new **target aspect ratio**: 16:9 (widescreen), 9:16
(vertical), or 1:1 (square). It's fast, but not perfect, tracking can lose the subject during a
quick cut or a crowded shot, and every Auto Reframe pass needs a human check afterward. **Clip
nesting** (treating a whole sequence as one clip) makes reframing a multi-shot sequence at once
possible instead of reframing every single clip separately.

> "Auto Reframe gets you 90 percent of the way there in a fraction of the time. The last 10
> percent, catching where it guessed wrong, is still an editor's job."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What does Auto Reframe actually track to decide how to reposition the frame?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might Auto Reframe struggle with a shot that has two people moving in
> opposite directions?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "A client wants five different platform versions of the same video.
> What's your workflow, and where do you expect to need to step in manually?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Open the provided 16:9 sequence** and nest it as a single clip.
2. **Run Auto Reframe** to produce a 9:16 version. Check every cut for tracking errors.
3. **Repeat for 1:1.** Compare which aspect ratio needed more manual correction.
4. **Fix any tracking errors by hand** using the Transform effect from 8.3.
5. **Small-group report-out:** which target aspect ratio was hardest, and why?

{: .discussion }
Where did Auto Reframe's motion tracking get it wrong, and what did you have to catch by hand?
