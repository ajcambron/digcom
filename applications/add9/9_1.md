# 9.1 | Sequence QC: Gaps, Levels & Safe Margins

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-sequence-qc.md %}

Before anything gets delivered, it gets checked. A quality-check pass is the difference between
"I think this is done" and "I confirmed this is actually ready."

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-9-1" title="9.1 Guided Notes: Sequence QC" presentation="/assets/presentations/add9-1.html" %}

A systematic QC pass checks, in order: **gaps in the timeline** (accidental black/silent stretches
from a Lift edit or a moved clip), **audio levels** (no clipping, nothing inaudibly quiet),
**safe margins** (is any text or critical action too close to the frame edge to survive a crop or
reframe), and a scan for a stray **black frame** at the head or tail.

> "'I think it's fine' is not a QC pass. Actually scrub the whole timeline, actually watch the
> levels meter, actually check the edges of the frame. Every time, not just when something feels
> off."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What are the four things a QC pass checks, in order?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might a gap in the timeline be easy to miss during editing but obvious to a
> viewer?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "You're QC-ing a sequence with no obvious problems. What would make you
> still not trust it yet?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Open the provided sequence** (5+ planted QC errors: gaps, audio issues, safe-margin
 violations, black frames).
2. **Run a full QC pass** in order: gaps, then audio, then safe margins, then a black-frame scan.
3. **Fix every error you find.**
4. **Partner check:** compare error lists independently, then discuss anything one of you missed.

{: .discussion }
Which type of error was easiest to miss, and why do you think that is?
