# 6.1 | Effect Presets & Adjustment Layers

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-effect-presets.md %}

Applying the same effect to 20 clips one at a time is slow and inconsistent. Today's tools —
presets, adjustment layers, and copy/paste attributes — are how editors avoid doing that.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add6-effect-presets-reference" title="6.1 Guided Notes: Effect Presets & Adjustment Layers" presentation="/assets/presentations/add6-1.html" %}

> "An adjustment layer doesn't touch your original clips at all — it sits above everything in the
> stack and applies its effect to whatever's beneath it. Delete the adjustment layer, and your
> clips are exactly as they were."

Walk through: applying an **effect preset**, creating and using an **adjustment layer**, **nesting**
a sequence for group effects, and **copy/paste attributes** to move settings between clips fast.

**Scaffolded questions — cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between applying an effect directly to a clip and using an
> adjustment layer?"

> **Analysis:** "Why would copy/paste attributes save more time on a 20-clip sequence than a
> 3-clip one?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If you wanted to apply one effect to only 3 of your 10 clips, would an
> adjustment layer still be the right tool? Why or why not?"

## Guided Practice — Apply & Reuse Effects (~23 min)

1. **Create an adjustment layer** and place it above your entire sequence.
2. **Apply an effect** to the adjustment layer and confirm it affects every clip beneath it.
3. **Remove the adjustment layer**, then apply that same effect directly to one individual clip
   instead.
4. **Copy that clip's attributes** and paste them onto 2 other clips, adjusting settings slightly
   on each.
5. **Partner comparison:** compare your adjustment-layer result to your copy/paste result — what's
   different about how each one behaves?

{: .discussion }
**Cold call check:** you need to remove an effect from your entire sequence at once. Which
approach from today makes that a one-step fix?
