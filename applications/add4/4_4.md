# 4.4 | Color Correction & Grading

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-color-grading.md %}

Color work happens in two distinct steps, in a specific order: fix the problem first, then make
the creative choice. Skip straight to grading and you're building a look on a broken foundation.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add4-color-grading-reference" title="4.4 Guided Notes: Color Correction & Grading" presentation="/assets/presentations/add4-4.html" %}

> "Correction is about accuracy—getting back to neutral. Grading is about intention—deciding
> what mood you actually want. Always correct first. You can't reliably grade footage that's
> still color-broken."

Walk through: **white balance**, **color correction** (Lumetri's Basic Correction panel), **color
grading** (Lumetri's Creative panel and Color Wheels), and **LUTs** as fast starting points for a
grade.

One quick color-theory reminder before you grade: warm tones (orange, amber) read as
*advancing* and energetic, while cool tones (teal, blue) read as *receding* and calm—that's why
so many film looks push shadows cool and highlights warm (the "teal and orange" look). Keep that
in mind when you're deciding what mood a grade should create.

**Scaffolded questions—cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between color correction and color grading?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does correction always need to happen before grading, not after?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If a LUT gets you 80% of the way to the look you want, what should
> you do with the other 20%?"

## Guided Practice—Correct & Grade (~23 min)

1. **Open the provided practice footage** in Premiere and apply the Lumetri Color effect.
2. **Correct first:** use Basic Correction to neutralize any off-color cast—aim for accurate
   white balance and exposure.
3. **Grade second:** apply an intentional look using the Creative panel or Color Wheels, choosing
   a specific mood.
4. **Partner critique:** compare graded looks with a partner and explain what mood each choice
   creates.
5. **Export a before/after/graded** comparison.

{: .discussion }
**Cold call check:** a classmate skips straight to grading without correcting first. What problem
are they likely to run into?
