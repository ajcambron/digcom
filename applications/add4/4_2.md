# 4.2 | Shot Analysis & Camera Technique

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-camera-technique.md %}

Great cinematography isn't random. It's a small set of deliberate choices (move, angle, framing,
exposure) made over and over. Today you learn to name them, then make them yourself.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add4-camera-technique-reference" title="4.2 Guided Notes: Shot Analysis & Camera Technique" presentation="/assets/presentations/add4-2.html" %}

> "Every camera move has a name and a reason: a dolly-in draws the audience closer to a moment
> that matters. A high angle makes a subject look small or vulnerable. None of this is accidental
> in professional work. And it doesn't need to be accidental in yours either."

Walk through: **camera moves** (pan, tilt, dolly, truck, pedestal), **camera angles** (neutral,
low, high, Dutch), **framing** (ECU through extreme long shot), and the basics of **exposure**
(aperture, shutter angle, gain).

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between a pan and a truck?"

> **Analysis:** "Why would a director choose a low angle to shoot a powerful character?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If you wanted to make an ordinary moment feel tense using only camera
> technique, no dialogue, no music, what would you do?"

## Guided Practice | Analyze & Replicate (~23 min)

1. **Choose a clip** and break down at least 5 distinct camera setups, labeling each by **angle**
 and **framing**.
2. **With a partner, discuss** what each setup's technique choice contributes to the shot.
3. **Using your own camera, replicate 3 different setups**: vary the angle, framing, or move
 across all three so they're clearly distinct.
4. **Check your exposure** on each setup before recording.

{: .discussion }
**Cold call check:** you shoot the exact same conversation from a high angle vs. a low angle.
What changes about how the audience feels toward each character?
