# 4.3 | Storyboard & Shot List

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-storyboard-shotlist.md %}

A video essay lives or dies on sequencing—which means today's planning work matters as much as
anything you'll shoot. No polished drawing skills required, just a clear plan.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add4-storyboard-shotlist-reference" title="4.3 Guided Notes: Storyboard & Shot List" presentation="/assets/presentations/add4-3.html" %}

> "Your storyboard sketches don't need to be photorealistic—they need to be clear enough that
> your team knows exactly what shot you mean. Spend an hour on this, not a day."

Walk through: **storyboarding** as pre-visualization (not art), building a **shot list** tied to
specific timing, and sourcing **royalty-free** or **public domain** music since S4's Video Essay
requires original or royalty-free audio only—no **synchronization rights** negotiation needed if
you pick correctly from the start.

**Scaffolded questions—cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between a storyboard and a shot list?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does the Video Essay summative require royalty-free or public domain music
> specifically, instead of any song you like?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If your storyboard and your shot list disagreed about a shot's timing,
> which would you trust more, and why?"

## Guided Practice—Plan Your Video Essay (~23 min)

1. **Storyboard at least 10 shots**, ensuring diversity in framing, depth of focus, and camera
   angle.
2. **Build a shot list** tied to specific timing or story beats.
3. **Source your music** from a royalty-free or public domain library (see the reference sheet)—confirm the license before committing to it.
4. **Peer storyboard review:** trade with a partner and check for real variety across all 10 shots.

{: .highlight }
If most of your 10 storyboarded shots are the same wide shot repeated, that's not diversity—push yourself to include close-ups, different angles, and varied depth of field.

{: .discussion }
**Cold call check:** you find the perfect song for your video essay, but it's not royalty-free or
public domain. What are your options?
