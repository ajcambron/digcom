# 4.1 | Video Styles

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-video-styles.md %}

Before you shoot anything this unit, you need a shared vocabulary for naming what you're making—because a video essay, a documentary, and a music video all use cinematography differently.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add4-video-styles-reference" title="4.1 Guided Notes: Video Styles" presentation="/assets/presentations/add4-1.html" %}

> "A video essay makes its argument through sequencing and sound alone—no narrator telling you
> what to think. A documentary leans on interviews and voiceover to guide you. Same tools, very
> different conventions."

Walk through each style by its defining convention: **video essay** (no narration, visual
argument), **documentary** (interview/VO-driven), **news package** (reporter-led, the Unit 3 form),
**music video** (performance/concept cut to a track), **PSA vs. commercial** (cause vs. product
persuasion), **narrative short** (scripted, the Unit 7 form).

**Scaffolded questions—cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the one convention that separates a video essay from a documentary?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might a filmmaker choose a video essay format over a documentary format for
> the same subject?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Pick a topic you care about. Which style would you use to cover it,
> and why is that the right fit over the other five?"

## Guided Practice—Classify the Clips (~23 min)

1. **In small groups, watch each assigned clip.**
2. **Classify its style** using today's vocabulary.
3. **Justify your classification** by citing a specific convention—not just a guess.
4. **Class report-out:** each group presents one clip's classification and justification.

{: .discussion }
**Cold call check:** a clip has no narration but IS scripted with actors and dialogue. Which style
is it, and how do you know?
