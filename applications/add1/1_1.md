# 1.1 | Intro to Digital Video Production

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-video-basics.md %}

Have you noticed the rise in amateur video productions — not festival entries, but videos made
by your neighbor's kids? Today, anyone with a computer, a smartphone, and editing software can
create a video that rivals what used to require an entire production studio.

## Part 1 — Guided Notes: Going Digital & the Movie-Making Process (~12 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add1-guided-notes-1-1" title="1.1 Guided Notes: Going Digital & the Movie-Making Process" presentation="https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1JSDCQZKT9Uqs2LbvCsVZIi2sCtcTqYR8ASGfuItKNJI/embed?start=false&loop=false&delayms=3000" %}

Every video, no matter how big or small, goes through four phases:

1. **Preproduction** — everything from the initial idea to scriptwriting. A shot list helps
   ensure every critical moment gets captured.
2. **Production** — capturing the footage. Follow the "three S's": **Straight, Steady, and
   Smooth** — a tripod helps with all three. Pay attention to lighting (avoid harsh backlighting
   and deep shadows) and keep the camera close enough to get clean audio.
3. **Postproduction** — this is where the film comes to life. Editing software imports footage,
   trims clips, and adds titles and effects — mirroring the tools professionals use.
4. **Distribution** — sharing the finished video: posting to a platform like YouTube, sending it
   directly to friends (compression may lower quality), sharing on social media, or backing it up
   to cloud storage.

{: .discussion }
Once you start paying attention to camera angles, sound, and effects in videos you watch for fun,
you start noticing them everywhere — has that already started happening to you?

## Part 2 — Movie-Making Process Autopsy (~48 min)

Your teacher will play two short, professional-quality videos. For each one, you'll fill out a
process chart identifying moments that reveal each of the four phases — and any technical choices
(frame rate, aspect ratio, codec) you can spot.

[![Sample Video 1](http://img.youtube.com/vi/TZohrS7MU20/0.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TZohrS7MU20){:target="_blank"}
[![Sample Video 2](http://img.youtube.com/vi/TjQ9c7ZcgzQ/0.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TjQ9c7ZcgzQ){:target="_blank"}

1. **Watch (silent first pass):** experience the whole video once without taking notes.
2. **Analyze (second pass):** watch again, pausing as needed, and fill in your worksheet —
   what evidence do you see of preproduction planning? Of deliberate production choices? Of
   postproduction editing? How is this video meant to be distributed (broadcast, streaming,
   social)?
3. **Partner check:** compare your chart with a neighbor's before the whole-class discussion.

{: .discussion }
Which phase's evidence was hardest to spot on screen — and why do you think that is?

## Wrap-Up (~8 min)

{: .reflection }
Think of a video you've personally seen or made. Walk through its four phases — what do you
think happened in each one?
