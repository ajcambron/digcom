# 1.2 | Topic Selection & Design Brief

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-client-audience.md %}

For this project, imagine you're being asked to create an original **explainer video** for an
educational YouTube channel — on a topic you're an expert on. Your final video must include an
introduction and a conclusion, use voice-over throughout to keep viewers engaged, and run
**1–3 minutes**.

## Opening Discussion

As a class, discuss popular explainer videos you've seen. Watch a couple of examples together,
then discuss:

- What is each video doing effectively?
- What could be improved?
- What topics do you think work well for an explainer video, and why?

## Part 1 — Guided Notes: Client Goals & Target Audience (~12 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add1-guided-notes-1-2" title="1.2 Guided Notes: Client Goals & Target Audience" presentation="placeholder" %}

Every project — even a school project — has a **client** with **goals**, and a **target
audience** with its own **demographics**. Before you pick a topic, you need to know who you're
making this video *for*.

A strong topic passes three tests:

1. **Relevance** — connects to your audience's interests or the assignment's purpose. Does it
   fulfill your design brief's criteria? Does it connect to something current or meaningful?
2. **Clarity** — sets specific boundaries and avoids vagueness. Is a broad idea narrowed down into
   something focused and easy to understand?
3. **Engagement** — captures attention and provokes curiosity. Is it something surprising,
   debatable, or personally meaningful?

{: .discussion }
What misconceptions can arise from assuming any topic is suitable without considering the
audience's interests?

## Part 2 — Topic Brainstorm & Design Brief (~48 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="topic-brainstorm-add1" title="Topic & Audience Brainstorm" %}

1. **Brainstorm (~10 min):** list 3–5 possible topics — things you genuinely consider yourself an
   expert on. For each, check whether it passes Relevance, Clarity, and Engagement, and note who
   its target audience would be.
2. **Select (~2 min):** circle your strongest topic. Ask a neighbor if you're not sure — a second
   opinion helps.
3. **Design Brief (~36 min):** complete a full design brief for your chosen topic. Use the
   sample below as your reference format, then fill out your own on the worksheet.

{: .reflection }

satisfy? How did you fix it?
