# 1.4 | Film Form & Shot Vocabulary

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-shot-types.md %}

**Film form** is the set of deliberate choices a filmmaker makes about framing and distance — not
accidents, but decisions that shape how an audience feels about a shot. Today you'll learn the
standard shot-type vocabulary, then apply it directly to your own script from 1.3.

## Part 1 — Guided Notes: Film Form & Shot Vocabulary (~12 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add1-guided-notes-1-4" title="1.4 Guided Notes: Film Form & Shot Vocabulary" presentation="placeholder" %}

{% include worksheet.html file="shot-vocabulary-reference" title="Shot Vocabulary Reference" %}

Shot types are named by how much of the subject they show — from tightest to widest:

- **ECU** (Extreme Close-Up) → **CU** (Close-Up) → **MCU** (Medium Close-Up) → **MS**
  (Medium Shot) → **WS** (Wide Shot) → **LS** (Long Shot) → **ELS** (Extreme Long Shot)

Three more shots are defined by purpose, not distance:

- **OTS** (Over-the-Shoulder) — frames one person from behind another's shoulder, common in
  dialogue.
- **POV** (Point of View) — shows exactly what a character sees, from their own eye line.
- **Cutaway** — a brief shot of something other than the main action, used for context or to
  hide an edit.

{: .discussion }
Why would a director choose an ECU instead of a WS for the same story moment? What feeling does
each create?

## Part 2 — Label Your Shot List (~48 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="shot-list-planner" title="Shot List Planner" %}

1. **Turn-and-talk (~5 min):** quiz a partner on shot-type abbreviations using the reference
   card — cover the names and see who can name the abbreviation from the description, and back.
2. **Label (~35 min):** walk down your two-column script from 1.3, row by row. For each shot,
   assign it a shot type from the reference card and note its location. Vary your shots on
   purpose — don't make everything a WS.
3. **Sanity-check (~8 min):** trade shot lists with a neighbor. Does each labeled shot type make
   sense for what's actually happening in that row of the script?

{: .highlight }
Include at least one **OTS** or **cutaway** shot somewhere in your list — you'll shoot this list
directly on your S | 1 project day.

## Wrap-Up (~8 min)

{: .reflection }
Pick one shot from your list. Why did you choose that specific shot type, and what feeling is it
meant to create?
