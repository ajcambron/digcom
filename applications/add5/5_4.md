# 5.4 | Essential Sound: Mixing & Repair

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-essential-sound.md %}

Recording clean audio was step one. Today's step two: mixing it into something that actually
sounds like a finished podcast, not a raw take with music dumped on top.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add5-essential-sound-reference" title="5.4 Guided Notes: Essential Sound" presentation="/assets/presentations/add5-4.html" %}

> "Auto Ducking is the single biggest 'sounds professional' move you can make in five minutes. > music that automatically steps back under dialogue and comes back up between lines is the
> difference between amateur and podcast-ready."

Walk through Premiere's **Essential Sound panel**: **Enhance Speech** (clarity/noise reduction),
**Loudness Auto-Match** (consistent levels across clips), **Auto Ducking** (music under dialogue),
**Repair** (fixing hum/reverb/noise), and **Remix** (fitting music to a target length).

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What does Auto Ducking actually do?"

> **Analysis:** "Why is Loudness Auto-Match especially important when you've recorded multiple
> clips at different times?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If Enhance Speech and Repair both claim to reduce noise, when would
> you reach for one over the other?"

## Guided Practice | Mix Your Segment (~23 min)

1. **Import your 5.3 voice-over** and a music track into a new sequence.
2. **Apply Enhance Speech** to your dialogue clip.
3. **Set up Auto Ducking** so the music lowers automatically under your voice-over.
4. **Apply Loudness Auto-Match** if you have multiple dialogue clips at different volumes.
5. **Add fade handles** for a clean fade-in at the start and fade-out at the end.
6. **Partner listening check:** play it for a partner. Is dialogue ever hard to hear under the
 music?

{: .discussion }
**Cold call check:** your mix sounds great through headphones but muddy on a phone speaker. What
Essential Sound tool would you revisit first?
