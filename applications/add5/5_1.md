# 5.1 | Podcast Production Planner

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-podcast-planning.md %}

Every good podcast episode starts the same way a good news story does: a specific question, clear
scope, and a crew who knows their jobs before anyone hits record.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add5-podcast-planning-reference" title="5.1 Guided Notes: Podcast Production Planner" presentation="/assets/presentations/add5-1.html" %}

> "A driving question is not the same as a topic. 'AI and art' is a topic. 'Is AI-generated art
> actually art?' is a driving question. It gives your episode something to actually resolve."

Walk through: defining a **driving question**, building a **"Not" list** to protect your runtime,
assigning **talent** (interviewer/guest), and assigning **production roles** (floor director,
video engineer, audio engineer).

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between a topic and a driving question?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does a 'Not' list help keep a 3-5 minute episode focused?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If your group can't agree on a driving question, how would you decide
> between two strong options?"

## Guided Practice | Plan Your Episode (~23 min)

1. **Choose a topic** (an AI-and-creativity angle works well, but any well-scoped topic is fine).
2. **Define your driving question** and your **"Not" list** as a group.
3. **Assign talent:** who's the interviewer, who's the guest(s)?
4. **Assign production roles:** floor director, video engineer, audio engineer.
5. **Complete the Podcast Planner document** together, then each member submits individually.

{: .discussion }
**Cold call check:** your group's "topic" is still just a broad subject after 10 minutes of
discussion. What question could you ask yourselves to force it narrower?
