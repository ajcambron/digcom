# 2.2 | Video Editing Basics

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-editing-basics.md %}

Non-linear editing means every edit you make is really just an instruction. The original
footage never changes, so you can rearrange, trim, and undo endlessly without losing quality.
Today you'll learn the core moves editors make hundreds of times an hour.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add2-editing-basics-reference" title="2.2 Guided Notes: Video Editing Basics" presentation="/assets/presentations/add2-2.html" %}

> "J, K, L. Backward, pause, forward. Tap L twice for double speed. This is how every professional
> editor moves through footage, because reaching for a mouse every time is too slow."

Walk through: the **timeline**, the **playhead**, **JKL navigation**, setting **In and Out points**
with `I` and `O`, and the four ways to get a clip onto the timeline. **Insert** (pushes everything
later), **Overwrite** (records over what's there), **Lift** (removes, leaves a gap), **Extract**
(removes, closes the gap).

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What do the In and Out points on a clip actually control?"

> **Analysis:** "Why would an editor choose Overwrite instead of Insert when they know exactly
> where a new clip should go?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If you Lift a clip out of the middle of your timeline by accident,
> what's the fastest way to notice and fix it?"

## Guided Practice | Assemble a Rough Cut (~23 min)

1. **Open your Unit 2 project** from 2.1 and confirm your bins are still organized.
2. **Set In and Out points** on at least 4 clips from your bins.
3. **Insert** your first 2 clips onto the timeline in order.
4. **Overwrite** your next clip directly into the middle of the sequence at a specific point.
5. **Practice a Lift and an Extract** on a clip you no longer want, and compare what each leaves
 behind.
6. **Turn and Talk:** trade seats with a partner and shuttle through each other's sequence using
 only JKL. Can you tell where each edit type was used?

{: .highlight }
If your sequence has an unexpected gap, that's almost always a Lift, not an Extract. Check what
type of edit created it before you fix it.

{: .discussion }
**Cold call check:** you need to remove a clip from the middle of a finished sequence and don't
want a gap left behind. Which command do you reach for, and why?
