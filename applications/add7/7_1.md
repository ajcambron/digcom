# 7.1 | Script Formatting & Storyboarding

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-script-formatting.md %}

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-storyboard-shotlist.md %}

Every short film starts as words on a page, formatted a specific way so a director, camera
operator, and editor can all read it fast and agree on what happens next.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add7-script-storyboard-reference" title="7.1 Guided Notes: Script Formatting" presentation="/assets/presentations/add7-1.html" %}

Hollywood convention exists so a script reads consistently no matter who wrote it. **Font** is
Courier or Courier New, 12-point (a monospaced font makes 1 page roughly equal 1 minute of screen
time). **Margins**: shot descriptions run a full 1 inch, dialogue insets to 2 inches with the
character's name centered above it. Every scene opens with a **slugline** in this exact order.
special information (flashback, slow-motion) first, then INT. or EXT., the location, shot type,
subject, and time of day last.

Three things get written in **ALL CAPS**: a speaking character's name the first time they appear,
sound effects or off-screen sounds, and camera direction. **Parentheticals** tell an actor how to
deliver a line, used sparingly. Once you have a formatted scene, break it into a **shot list**.
every shot the camera needs, numbered in order, then sketch each one as a rough **storyboard**
panel: stick figures are fine, the point is composition, not art.

> "A slugline out of order isn't just messy, it's a crew member on set not knowing where or when
> a scene happens until they've read the whole line twice. Order exists so nobody has to guess."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What order do the parts of a slugline go in?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might a two-column script work better than standard screenplay format for a
> music video or commercial?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "You have a 3-page script. Roughly how many shots would you expect to
> need, and why isn't that the same as the number of scenes?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Format a short scene** (provided, unformatted) to spec: Courier 12pt, correct margins,
 sluglines in the right order, ALL CAPS where required.
2. **Break the scene into a shot list**: number every shot the camera needs (15-30 shots for a
 2-6 minute film is the target range for the final short film, this scene will be shorter).
3. **Storyboard each shot**: a quick sketch per shot noting shot type, camera movement (if any),
 and a one-line action summary.
4. **Turn and Talk:** trade with a partner. Do your storyboard panels tell the story without the
 script? If not, what's missing?

Which was harder to plan for: what the camera sees, or what the audience hears?
