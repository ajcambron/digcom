# 7.4 | Releases, Rights & Budget

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-production-legal.md %}

A finished film that nobody legally has permission to show isn't finished. this lesson covers the
paperwork and budgeting that turn a shoot plan into something you can actually publish.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add7-rights-budget-reference" title="7.4 Guided Notes: Releases, Rights & Budget" presentation="/assets/presentations/add7-4.html" %}

Every on-camera performer needs a **talent release**, even a friend doing a favor, even a
classmate. Every non-public filming location needs a **location release**. Any music you didn't
write yourself needs cleared **usage rights**: **royalty-free** (one-time fee or free, reusable
without ongoing payments), **public domain** (no copyright at all), or a proper **synchronization
right** if you're pairing someone else's copyrighted song with your video.

A real budget breaks costs into **line items**. gear rental, location fees, talent/catering,
post-production, music licensing. and always includes a **contingency** line, roughly 10 percent
of the total, for the reshoot or broken prop you didn't plan for.

> "'We'll just ask for permission later if anyone notices' is not a rights strategy. Get the
> release signed before you roll camera, not after you've already used the footage."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between a talent release and a location release?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might a production choose royalty-free music over a popular copyrighted
> song, even if the copyrighted song fits the scene better?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Your budget is 15 percent over what your group can actually spend.
> Which line item would you cut first, and what would you lose by cutting it?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **List every performer and location** from your call sheet (7.3) and draft a release for each.
2. **Decide your music plan**: original, royalty-free, or public domain, and note which.
3. **Build a line-item budget**: gear, locations, talent/catering, post-production, music, plus a
 contingency line (about 10 percent of the total).
4. **Cross-check** your budget and releases against your shooting schedule. Does every location
 and performer on the schedule have a matching release?

{: .discussion }
Which line item in your budget was hardest to estimate, and why?
