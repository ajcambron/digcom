# 7.3 | Production Plan

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-production-legal.md %}

A script tells you what happens. A production plan tells you when, where, and who needs to show
up to make it happen, in an order that actually saves time.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-7-3" title="7.3 Guided Notes: Production Plan" presentation="/assets/presentations/add7-3.html" %}

A **shooting schedule** almost never matches story order. Instead, group scenes by **location**
(film everything in one place before moving, even if those scenes happen at different points in
the story) and by **actor availability** (if an actor can only come Saturday, every scene with
them gets scheduled that day). A **call sheet** is the daily document everyone actually checks:
which scenes are shooting, call time, location, and who (cast, crew) needs to be there.

A **plan-of-work log** tracks the plan as it changes. shoots get rescheduled, locations fall
through, and a written record of what actually happened (not just what was planned) is what makes
a production book (Unit 9) possible to write later.

> "If your call sheet just says 'film the movie Saturday,' it's not a call sheet, it's a wish.
> A real call sheet tells a specific person to be at a specific place at a specific time for a
> specific reason."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What two things do you group scenes by when building a shooting schedule?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might filming out of story order actually make it harder to keep continuity
> (matching hair, costume, prop positions) consistent?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Your lead actor can only shoot on one weekend day. How does that
> constraint change your entire shooting schedule?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **List every scene** from your group's script (from 7.1's shot list) with its location and
 which cast members appear in it.
2. **Group scenes by location**, then check for actor-availability conflicts within each group.
3. **Build your shooting schedule**: the order you'll actually film in, with a one-sentence reason
 for the grouping.
4. **Fill out a Day 1 call sheet**: location, call time, scenes shooting, cast/crew needed.

{: .discussion }
What could go wrong on your Day 1 shoot that the call sheet wouldn't catch, and how would your
group handle it?
