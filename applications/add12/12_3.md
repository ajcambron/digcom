# 12.3 | Music Video: Mise-en-Scène & Planning

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-visual-reference.md %}

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-script-formatting.md %}

A music video doesn't have dialogue to script, it has a song. Today's about pairing image to
music with the same deliberateness you'd bring to any other script.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add12-music-video-reference" title="12.3 Guided Notes: Music Video Planning" presentation="/assets/presentations/add12-3.html" %}

A **two-column script** splits visuals (left) from lyrics/musical cues (right), so image and
sound are planned together instead of one added as an afterthought. **Mise-en-scène** covers
everything deliberately arranged in frame: **settings and props**, **costume/hair/makeup**,
**lighting** (high-key, low-key, natural), and **color**. Every one of these is a mood decision,
not just a production logistics decision.

> "A verse and a chorus usually need to feel different, that's often where mise-en-scène does the
> most work: a color shift, a lighting change, a costume detail that only shows up once the
> chorus hits."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's on each side of a two-column script?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might a music video use natural lighting for a verse and stylized, low-key
> lighting for the chorus?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Your chosen song shifts mood halfway through. What mise-en-scène
> element would you change to reflect that shift, and why that one specifically?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Choose a song** (royalty-free or public domain, per 7.4's rights framework).
2. **Build a two-column script** for the first verse and chorus, pairing visuals to lyrics/cues.
3. **Plan mise-en-scène** for that section: settings, costume, lighting, color, with a reason for
 each choice.
4. **Small-group pitch:** present your plan to another group and get feedback.

{: .discussion }
Which mise-en-scène element did the most work to set your song's mood?

## Studio/Project Work Time (~30 min)

Storyboard your planned section, then shoot a quick phone-camera test clip of one shot to check
whether your mise-en-scène plan actually reads on camera.

## Exit Ticket / Wrap-Up (~5 min)

Save your file, then screenshot your script and plan.
