# 3.4 | Captions & Accessibility

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-captions-accessibility.md %}

A huge share of video is watched muted—social feeds, waiting rooms, classrooms. Captions aren't
an afterthought; they're how most people will actually experience your story.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="add3-captions-accessibility-reference" title="3.4 Guided Notes: Captions & Accessibility" presentation="/assets/presentations/add3-4.html" %}

> "Auto-captions get you most of the way there fast—but names, technical terms, and background
> noise trip them up every time. A human proofread pass is not optional if accuracy matters."

Walk through: **captions** vs. **subtitles** vs. **audio descriptions**, generating a **transcript**
with **auto transcribing**, proofreading for accuracy, and checking **color contrast** for
readability.

**Scaffolded questions—cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between captions and subtitles?"

> **Analysis:** "Why can't you trust auto-generated captions without a human proofread pass?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Your news story will be watched both on TV (sound on) and on social
> media (often muted). How does that change what 'accessibility' means for this one piece?"

## Guided Practice—Caption Your Story (~23 min)

1. **Auto-generate captions** on your 3.3 edit.
2. **Proofread every line**—fix misheard names, technical terms, and punctuation.
3. **Check color contrast**—make sure captions stay readable against every background in your
   footage, adjusting style if needed.
4. **Export two deliverables:** a version with captions burned in, and a separate sidecar caption
   file.
5. **Partner proofread:** swap with a partner and catch anything the other missed.

{: .discussion }
**Cold call check:** your auto-captions spelled your interview subject's name wrong throughout.
What's the real-world cost of shipping that without fixing it?
