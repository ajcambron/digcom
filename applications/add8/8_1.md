# 8.1 | What Does an Editor Do?

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-editorial-craft.md %}

Anyone can drag clips onto a timeline in order. Editing is the judgment calls underneath that.
which take, how long to hold it, and when to cut away, decisions the raw footage doesn't make for
you.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-8-1" title="8.1 Guided Notes: What Does an Editor Do?" presentation="/assets/presentations/add8-1.html" %}

**Editorial judgment** is every decision that isn't dictated by the footage itself: which of five
takes of the same line, whether to hold a reaction shot half a second longer, when to cut away
from the speaker to show what they're reacting to. **Continuity editing** keeps time, space, and
action feeling seamless even though the footage was shot out of order from multiple angles. The
**invisible edit** is the goal most of the time: a cut the audience doesn't consciously notice,
often by **cutting on action**, making the cut mid-movement so the motion itself hides it.

> "A good edit isn't the one people compliment. It's the one nobody notices, because it never
> pulled them out of the story to think about the editing at all."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between assembling clips and editing them?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does cutting in the middle of a movement, instead of before or after it, make
> a cut harder to notice?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "You have two equally good takes of the same line. What besides
> performance quality might decide which one you use?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Open the provided raw-takes folder** for one short scene, multiple angles, multiple takes.
2. **Build a first cut**, choosing takes and cut points deliberately, not just in the order
 they're numbered.
3. **Check continuity**: does position, prop placement, and action match across every cut?
4. **Try one cut on action**: find a movement in the scene and cut in the middle of it instead of
 before or after.
5. **Turn and Talk:** defend your trickiest cut choice to a partner before finalizing.

{: .discussion }
Which cut in your scene took the most judgment to make, and what did you base the decision on?
