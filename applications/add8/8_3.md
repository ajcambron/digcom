# 8.3 | Transform, Resize & Speed

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-transform-speed.md %}

A clip's frame size and speed aren't fixed once it's shot. Today's about the tools that reshape
both, on purpose, without wrecking quality.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-8-3" title="8.3 Guided Notes: Transform, Resize & Speed" presentation="/assets/presentations/add8-3.html" %}

The **Transform** effect controls **scale**, **rotation**, and position. Scaling past 100 percent
stretches existing pixels rather than adding detail, so quality drops fast. When a clip's aspect
ratio doesn't match your sequence, you have options besides a hard crop: **letterboxing**
(preserve the whole frame, add bars), or **Generative Expand** (AI-fill new pixels at the edges).

On the time side: **Rate Stretch** changes speed by dragging a clip's edge directly in the
timeline; **time remapping** lets speed change gradually or at keyframed points within one clip; a
**freeze frame** holds a single frame while playback continues around it.

> "Scaling a clip up to 150 percent to 'fix' a bad framing choice on set doesn't fix anything, it
> just makes the quality problem visible at a bigger size. Reframe in-camera when you can."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What happens to image quality when you scale a clip past 100 percent?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might letterboxing be a better choice than cropping for a shot with
> important action near the frame's edges?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "You need the same 16:9 interview clip to work as a 9:16 vertical
> post. What's your plan, and what will you lose?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Open the provided 16:9 clip** and a sequence set to a different aspect ratio.
2. **Reframe it** using the Transform effect, keeping the subject in frame with minimal quality
 loss.
3. **Apply one speed effect**: Rate Stretch, time remapping, or a freeze frame, with a clear
 reason for the choice.
4. **Small-group report-out:** which target aspect ratio required the most creative trade-offs?

{: .discussion }
What content got cropped out of frame when you reframed your clip? Did it matter to the shot?
