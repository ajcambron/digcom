# 8.2 | Pacing

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/add-editorial-craft.md %}

Two edits of the same footage, using the exact same shots, can feel completely different. The
only thing that changed is pacing.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-8-2" title="8.2 Guided Notes: Pacing" presentation="/assets/presentations/add8-2.html" %}

**Rhythm** is the felt pace of a sequence, created by shot length and how the space between cuts
changes over time. Shortening shot length generally raises **tension**; holding a shot longer
lets a moment breathe. A **montage** compresses time through many short shots; a long, unbroken
take does the opposite, forcing the audience to sit inside a moment in real time. Neither is
better, the choice depends entirely on what the scene needs to feel like.

> "Count the cuts in a 30-second action scene versus a 30-second conversation sometime. The
> action scene probably has five times as many. That's not an accident, it's pacing doing its
> job."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What generally happens to tension when you shorten average shot length?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might a director deliberately use one long, unbroken take instead of cutting
> it into a fast montage, even for a tense moment?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Your scene needs to feel like time is running out. Besides shortening
> shots, what else about pacing could you change to reinforce that?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Open the provided sequence** and source clips.
2. **Build a fast-paced version**: shorter average shot length, quicker cuts.
3. **Build a slow-paced version** of the same footage: longer holds, fewer cuts.
4. **Compare the two.** What does each version make you feel that the other doesn't?
5. **Turn and Talk:** screen both for a partner without saying which is which. Can they guess
 which one you intended as "fast"?

{: .discussion }
Think about a scene from your own Short Film footage (Unit 7). Would it work better paced fast
or slow, and why?
