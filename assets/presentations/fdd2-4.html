Rasterizing vs. Rendering

Rasterizing converts a vector object into a fixed grid of pixels. It's a one-way trip: afterward you can't edit it as clean paths.
Rendering is the general process of a program calculating and displaying an image, on screen or in an exported file.

FDD 2.4 · Kandinsky & Itten
Kandinsky, Point and Line to Plane (1926): an acute angle (45°) reads yellow, a right angle (90°) red, an obtuse angle (135°) blue as it heads toward the circle. His 1923 Bauhaus questionnaire asked people to color a triangle, square, and circle, and the classic answer was yellow, red, blue. Itten taught the three form characters in the foundation course (square: horizontal and vertical, tense; triangle: diagonal, every kind of angle; circle: one even, continuous movement) and paired them with the same three colors in The Art of Color. Bridge: in the Bauhaus Shape Exercise, act out each form before you sketch it. Which character fits your portfolio site?

Rulers, Grids & Guides

After Otl Aicher, Munich 1972
Rulers ⌘R · Grid ⌘' · Snap ⇧⌘'
Guides: drag out of a ruler
Smart Guides ⌘U
Origin: drag the corner ●
FDD 2.4 · Rulers, Grids & Guides
Start with the pictogram: Otl Aicher drew every Munich 1972 Olympics pictogram on the same square grid with 45° diagonals, so arms, legs, and bodies only ever run horizontal, vertical, or at 45°, and every limb is the same width (it spans exactly two grid squares on the diagonal). Point to the bold cyan guide: the kicking leg and the edge of the shirt sit on the same 45° line, which is why the figure feels so unified. That's what guides are for: decisions made once and reused everywhere. Then the S2 workspace: Cmd+R for rulers, drag a guide to 24 px from the top ruler, set Preferences > Guides & Grid to 24 px with 4 subdivisions, Cmd+' to show the grid. The cyan guides match the notes: a 24 px live-area margin, the mark zone ends at 168 px, the type zone starts at 216 px. The favicon artboard (192 × 192) has the same 144 px mark area, so one mark fits both.
