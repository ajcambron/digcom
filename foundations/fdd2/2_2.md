## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-2-2" title="2.2 Guided Notes: The Illustrator Interface" presentation="/assets/presentations/fdd2-2.html" %}

Live in Illustrator, the teacher will point out each major zone in order: the **Application Bar**
at the very top, the **Control Panel** just below it (context-sensitive. It changes depending on
what's selected), the **Toolbar** running down the side, **Panels** docked to the right (Layers,
Swatches, Color, and more), and the **Document Window** in the center, showing your artboard
sitting inside the larger gray **Pasteboard**.

> "Watch what happens to the Control Panel when I select the Type tool versus when I select this
> rectangle. Same panel, completely different options, because it's context-sensitive."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between the Toolbar and a Panel?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might Illustrator make the Control Panel context-sensitive instead of just
> showing every possible option all the time?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If you were designing a totally new piece of software, what would you
> put in a 'contextual task bar' that follows the user's selection around?"

## GMetrix/BrainBuffet | Joining for the First Time (~53 min)

This runs the entire rest of class today.

Today's your first login. Before touching any module content, get set up:

1. **Open Schoology** and find today's GMetrix/BrainBuffet classroom join code, posted in the
 course materials.
2. **Go to the GMetrix/BrainBuffet site** (link posted alongside the code) and create an account
 using your **school email address**. Not a personal one.
3. **Enter the classroom code** to join our class. If it doesn't work, double-check for typos
 before asking. Codes are case-sensitive.
4. **Once you're in,** find **Module 1: Getting Started** and begin working through it. Today's
 sections build directly on the interface tour you just completed.

{: .highlight }
If your school-email verification email doesn't arrive, flag the teacher right away instead of
troubleshooting alone. First-day sign-up snags are common and worth solving live, not something
to quietly sit stuck on.
