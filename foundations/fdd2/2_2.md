Now that you know *why* Illustrator works the way it does, let's tour *where* everything lives on
screen.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-2-2" title="2.2 Guided Notes: The Illustrator Interface" presentation="placeholder" %}

Live in Illustrator, the teacher will point out each major zone in order: the **Application Bar**
at the very top, the **Control Panel** just below it (context-sensitive — it changes depending on
what's selected), the **Toolbar** running down the side, **Panels** docked to the right (Layers,
Swatches, Color, and more), and the **Document Window** in the center, showing your artboard
sitting inside the larger gray **Pasteboard**.

> "Watch what happens to the Control Panel when I select the Type tool versus when I select this
> rectangle — same panel, completely different options, because it's context-sensitive."

**Scaffolded questions — cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between the Toolbar and a Panel?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might Illustrator make the Control Panel context-sensitive instead of just
> showing every possible option all the time?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If you were designing a totally new piece of software, what would you
> put in a 'contextual task bar' that follows the user's selection around?"

## Guided Practice — Interface Scavenger Hunt (~23 min)

{: .highlight }
**Think-Pair-Share:** work with a partner for this whole activity.

1. **Partner A calls out an interface zone by name** (e.g., "Control Panel") — **Partner B** has
   5 seconds to point to it or click something inside it. Switch roles after each round; go
   through all 5 zones from the diagram at least once each.
2. **Customize your workspace:** drag one panel out of the dock to float freely, then drag it back
   to re-dock it. Try collapsing a panel to just its icon and expanding it again.
3. **Take a screenshot** of your full Illustrator window and add 5 text callouts labeling the
   Application Bar, Control Panel, Toolbar, Panels, and Document Window.

{: .discussion }
**Turn and Talk:** which flyout tool group (the ones hiding behind a small triangle) did you find
that you didn't know existed before today?
