# 2.4 | Design Aids: Rulers, Guides, Grids & Views

## Key Terms

{% include vocab/fdd-design-aids.md %}

You can build a document (2.3)—now let's make sure everything inside it lines up precisely.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-2-4" title="2.4 Guided Notes: Design Aids" presentation="/assets/presentations/fdd2-4.html" %}

Turn on **View > Rulers** (`Cmd/Ctrl+R`) and drag a **guide** out from the top ruler. Turn on
**Smart Guides** (`Cmd/Ctrl+U`) and drag a shape near another—notice the temporary alignment
hints Illustrator pops up on its own. Then toggle **View > Show Grid** and **Snap to Grid**.

Finally, cycle through the three view modes together: **Preview** (normal), **Outline**
(wireframe paths only—great for finding something hidden behind another object), and **Pixel
Preview** (shows how the art will rasterize on a screen). Briefly demo **Presentation Mode** as
well.

> "Watch what happens when I switch to Outline view—that shape I couldn't click a second ago,
> because it was hiding behind another one? Now I can see and select it."

**Scaffolded questions—cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between a guide and the grid?"

> **Analysis:** "Why would Outline view help you find an object that Preview mode can't?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If you were about to present your work to a client in the room, which
> view mode would you switch to right before showing them, and why?"

## Reading & Discussion—The Four Basic Principles (~8 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="fdd2-4-design-principles-discussion" title="2.4 Reading Discussion: The Four Basic Principles" %}

**In groups of 3-4:** read the handout together (Ch. 1 of Robin Williams' *The Non-Designer's
Design Book*), then discuss and record group answers to all 5 questions. One student per group
records. Every student should be ready to share their group's answer if cold-called.

> "Before you touch a single guide, I want you to understand *why* alignment matters—not just
> the button to click. This chapter's whole argument is that naming a principle is what gives you
> power over it."

The reading explains why naming a design principle gives you power over it. Guided Practice
immediately below has students apply one of those principles (Alignment) with Illustrator's own
tools.

## Guided Practice—Fix-This-File (~23 min)

1. **Trade starter files with a partner.** Each file has objects scattered with no alignment, at
   least one object hidden behind another, and no guides.
2. **Turn on rulers and Smart Guides**, then drag out guides to establish a simple layout grid.
3. **Realign every object** to your guides.
4. **Find the hidden object** using Outline view, then switch back to Preview once you've selected
   and moved it into place.
5. **Screenshot** your fixed file with guides visible, and write one sentence naming which view
   mode helped you find the hidden object.
6. **Explain to your partner** which design aid solved which problem in their file.

{: .discussion }
**Turn and Talk:** which was faster for you today—eyeballing alignment, or trusting Smart
Guides? Why?
