# 2.3 | Define the Problem & Research and Learn

{% include vocab/fdd-documents-artboards.md %}

Your Google Sites portfolio is live, with two empty slots waiting: a logo and a favicon. Today
starts the project that fills them. Before any sketching, a real design process starts by
**defining the problem** precisely and **researching** the actual constraints, then, and only
then, learning the tool well enough to set up a document that can hold the solution.

## Direct Instruction (~15 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-2-3-interface" title="2.3 Guided Notes: The Illustrator Interface" presentation="/assets/presentations/fdd2-3.html" %}

Live in Illustrator, the teacher will point out each major zone in order: the **Application Bar**
at the very top, the **Control Panel** just below it (context-sensitive—it changes depending on
what's selected), the **Toolbar** running down the side, **Panels** docked to the right (Layers,
Swatches, Color, and more), and the **Document Window** in the center, showing your artboard
sitting inside the larger gray **Pasteboard**.

> "Watch what happens to the Control Panel when I select the Type tool versus when I select this
> rectangle. Same panel, completely different options, because it's context-sensitive."

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-2-3-documents" title="2.3 Guided Notes: Documents & Artboards" %}

Click **File > New**. A document can hold more than one artboard, exactly what today's project
needs: **one file, two artboards**, a wide Header Logo and a square Favicon. Walk through
**Bleed**, **Trim**, and **Live Area** together: bleed and trim matter most for print (a logo
often gets reused later on a business card or signage, so it's a real professional habit even on
a digital-first project), but **live area** matters immediately, keeping a mark's important
details a safe margin from the edge so nothing feels cropped once the Favicon shrinks to 48px in
a browser tab.

> "A business card front and back live as two artboards in one file. Your logo project works the
> same way. Two sizes, two artboards, one file."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between the Toolbar and a Panel? And separately, what's the
> difference between an artboard and the pasteboard?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does live area matter more for a 48px favicon than it does for a full-size
> poster?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If Google Sites changed its favicon requirement tomorrow, what about
> today's document setup would make that easy to adapt to, and what would make it hard?"

## Guided Practice | Define the Problem (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="fdd2-3-design-brief" title="2.3 Design Brief: Geometric Logo" %}

Fill out the Design Brief together as a class, then finish your own Target Consumer and Design
Statement fields independently. This is not a formality, everything in Criteria and Constraints
becomes a real requirement your S2 project gets checked against.

{: .discussion }
Your Opportunity Statement names a specific problem: two empty slots on your own site. Why does
naming the *exact* problem (not just "I need a logo") make the rest of this project easier?

## Guided Practice | Research and Learn (~16 min)

1. **Research and Learn**: confirm the researched specs on your Design Brief, a header logo capped
 at 225×40px display (a 5.625:1 ratio) and a favicon built in multiples of 48px, square.
2. **File > New:** a document sized to comfortably work at those proportions, CMYK color, small
 bleed (0.125") for print-reuse habit, with **2 artboards**.
3. **Rename each artboard**: "Header Logo" and "Favicon," not the defaults.
4. **Turn on View > Show Bleed** and check that each artboard's proportions actually match the
 researched ratio (5.625:1 for Header Logo, 1:1 for Favicon).
5. **Check with a partner:** confirm each other's artboard names and proportions before moving on.
6. Save your file as `LastName_FirstName_2_3_Logo.ai`.

{: .discussion }
If Illustrator's default New Document sizes don't include your exact researched ratio, how do you
set one up correctly by hand instead?
