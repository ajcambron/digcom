# 4.1 | Art Analysis & Inspiration Board

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-anchor-points.md %}

Welcome to Unit 4 — for the next several classes, you'll design and build a **Low-Poly
Stained-Glass** piece in Adobe Illustrator using the Pen tool. Before you touch the software,
you're going to study the two art traditions your final piece will blend: the color and
storytelling of stained glass windows, and the geometric, faceted style of low poly art.

## Direct Instruction — Reading Art Like a Designer (~10 min)

> "Every curve you see in a finished piece of art started as a decision — where to place a point,
> and how far to pull its handles. Today we're learning to see those decisions."

The teacher will project one stained glass example (e.g. Chartres Cathedral or a Marc Chagall
window) and one low poly illustration, and think aloud through:

- **Color & Light** — where are the boldest colors? Where does the piece use contrast versus
  harmony?
- **Geometric Structure** — point out where a shape's outline would need a **corner point** (an
  abrupt change of direction) versus a **smooth point** (a continuous curve). Even a "simple"
  stained glass border is really a sequence of anchor points and segments.
- **Story/Symbolism** — what is this piece communicating, and how do the shapes support that?

{: .discussion }
**Recall:** What's the difference between a stained glass window and a low poly illustration, just
based on how each one is built? **Analysis:** Why might a designer choose sharp, angular facets
(low poly) to represent a subject that traditional stained glass usually renders with smooth
curves? **Synthesis:** If you were combining both styles in one piece, where would you keep sharp
corners, and where would you want a smooth curve?

## Guided Practice — Analyze & Curate (~23 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="fdd4-art-analysis-notecatcher" title="Art Analysis Note-Catcher" presentation="placeholder" %}

1. **Think-Pair-Share (~5 min):** with a partner, look at one reference image the teacher provides
   and complete the first row of your Note-Catcher together out loud.
2. **Independent research (~13 min):** search for **5–7 images** — a mix of stained glass and low
   poly art — that you find inspiring for your own piece. For each, fill in a row of your
   Note-Catcher.
3. **Build your board (~5 min):** start a digital inspiration board (Google Slides, Docs, or
   Canva) with your chosen images and a 2–3 sentence annotation under each, explaining:
   - What you find inspiring about the piece
   - How it will influence your final stained glass design
   - Any specific colors, shapes, or themes you want to incorporate

{: .highlight }
Use the words **anchor point**, **segment**, or **curve** in at least two of your annotations —
you'll be using this exact vocabulary to build the piece yourself starting next class.

## Exit Ticket / Wrap-Up (~5 min)

Save your inspiration board draft (you'll keep adding to it through 4.3) and submit your
Note-Catcher.

{: .reflection }
Which single reference image most influenced your early thinking about your own design, and why?
