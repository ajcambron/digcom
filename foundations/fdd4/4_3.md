# 4.3 | Concept Sketches

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-selection-tools.md %}

Today you'll plan the actual composition, color palette, and meaning behind your stained-glass
piece on paper — before you build a single path in Illustrator.

## Direct Instruction — Sketching a Composition (~10 min)

The teacher sketches a simple window composition on the board, narrating decisions aloud:

- **Panel structure** — where do the major dividing lines go? A rose window, a rectangular grid,
  and an arched panel all organize their shapes differently.
- **Color placement** — warm colors for energy or emphasis, cool colors for calm or background;
  contrast draws the eye, harmony holds a piece together.
- **Story/symbolism** — what does this composition need to communicate, and which shapes carry
  that meaning?

{: .discussion }
**Recall:** Name one thing stained glass windows across different cultures tend to have in common.
**Analysis:** Why might a designer put their most important shape at the center of a rose window
composition instead of in a corner? **Synthesis:** If your stained glass piece told a story in
three parts, how would you divide your panels to show that?

## Guided Practice — Plan Your Piece (~23 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="fdd4-concept-sketch-planner" title="Concept Sketch Planner" presentation="placeholder" %}

1. **Sketch (~10 min):** using your 4.1 inspiration board for reference, sketch your window
   composition — bold, clear panel divisions, with your main subject placed intentionally.
2. **Color palette (~6 min):** choose and swatch at least 4 colors inspired by stained glass
   traditions, considering warm/cool contrast and symbolic meaning.
3. **Written explanation (~7 min):** answer the Planner's 3 questions about the meaning behind
   your design, why you chose these colors and shapes, and how it connects to stained-glass or
   low-poly traditions.

{: .highlight }
Circle every place on your sketch where you'll need a **corner point** vs. a **smooth point** once
you rebuild it in Illustrator — you'll thank yourself next class.

## Exit Ticket / Wrap-Up (~5 min)

Photograph your finished sketch — you'll build directly from it in upcoming classes.

{: .reflection }
Peer exchange: briefly show a partner your color choices. Did explaining your reasoning out loud
change how confident you feel about them?
