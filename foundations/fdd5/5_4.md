# 5.4 | Text Flow & Converting to Outlines

Last lesson before S5. Today closes two loops: keeping longer text under control across multiple
shapes, and locking in finished type so it displays correctly no matter whose computer opens the
file.

## Direct Instruction — Live Demo (~10 min)

> "See that little red plus sign? That's Illustrator telling you there's more text than fits —
> and threading is how you tell it where the rest should go."

The teacher live-demos:

1. **Threading** — linking an overflowing text area to a second shape.
2. **Wrapping** — making a paragraph flow around a placed image instead of behind it.
3. **Converting to outlines** — turning a finished headline into vector paths.

{: .discussion }
**Recall:** What does the red plus icon mean? **Analysis:** Why would you wrap text around an
image instead of just leaving a manual gap by adjusting line breaks? **Synthesis:** Why is
converting text to outlines a one-way decision you should make *last*, only once the wording is
completely final?

## Guided Practice — Fix, Wrap, Outline (~23 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="fdd5-text-flow-reference" title="Text Flow & Outlines Reference" presentation="placeholder" %}

1. **Thread (~8 min):** open the provided file. Click its overflow icon, then click the empty
   linked shape to thread the remaining text into it.
2. **Wrap (~8 min):** place the provided image into your text, then apply Text Wrap so the
   paragraph flows around it instead of behind it.
3. **Outline (~7 min):** once your headline's wording is completely final, select it and convert
   it to outlines. Confirm it worked by checking that the Character panel commands are now grayed
   out for that text.

{: .highlight }
Converting text to outlines is permanent — you can no longer edit it as text afterward. Always
finish your wording and proofread *before* you outline.

## Exit Ticket / Wrap-Up (~5 min)

Screenshot your finished file: no overflow icon, image wrapped, headline outlined.

{: .reflection }
Why would a designer convert a logo's text to outlines before sending a file to a printer, but
never do that to a paragraph of body copy?
