# 5.2 | Character Settings

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-character-settings.md %}

Today you'll get hands-on with the **Character panel** — the single tool you'll reach for most
often to make type look intentional instead of just "default."

## Direct Instruction — Live Demo (~10 min)

> "Watch what happens to the word 'AVAILABLE' when I kern just the A and the V closer together —
> same letters, completely different feel."

The teacher live-demos on sample text:

1. **Kerning** — tightening a specific letter pair.
2. **Tracking** — loosening or tightening a whole headline evenly.
3. **Leading** — adjusting the vertical rhythm of a paragraph.
4. **Baseline shift** — nudging one character up for a superscript-style effect.

{: .discussion }
**Recall:** What's the difference between kerning and tracking? **Analysis:** Why does tightly
tracked text often work for a bold headline but feel cramped and hard to read in a paragraph?
**Synthesis:** If a client's logo wordmark had one awkward letter gap, which single setting would
you reach for first, and why?

## Guided Practice — Fix-This-File (~23 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="fdd5-character-settings-reference" title="Character Panel Quick Reference" presentation="placeholder" %}

1. **Fix-This-File (~13 min):** open the provided paragraph — it has **3 planted errors**
   (something like: two letters kerned too far apart, tracking cranked too tight to read, and
   leading so loose the paragraph falls apart). Find and fix all 3.
2. **Apply to your own text (~10 min):** type a short headline or quote of your own, and apply at
   least one deliberate kerning, tracking, or leading adjustment that improves it.

{: .highlight }
Horizontal and vertical scale are on the Quick Reference for a reason: **avoid them.** They
stretch a typeface's carefully designed letterforms out of proportion. If you want bolder or
narrower type, switch to a different font weight or style instead.
