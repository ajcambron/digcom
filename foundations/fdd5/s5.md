# S | 5 | Typographic Poster

Over the next several class meetings, you'll design and produce an original **typographic
poster** — a single finished piece where the type itself, not a photo or illustration, carries the
entire design.

## Project Idea

| Driving Question | How can typography alone — without photography or illustration — communicate a message, mood, and visual identity? |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Learning Goals** | Select and justify a typeface pairing using anatomy/classification vocabulary; refine Character panel settings for legibility and impact; use at least one special-type element (area type or type on a path); keep text flow clean with no overflow; convert final type to outlines. |
| **Project Summary** | Students design an original poster built entirely from typography, choosing a quote, topic, or message and expressing it through typeface choice, scale, color, and layout alone. |
| **Major Products** | A finished poster file and exported portfolio image; optionally, a printed poster for physical display. |
| **Making it Public** | Published to each student's portfolio website; printed posters displayed in a class gallery walk. |
| **Key Project Documents** | General Rubric for Individual Projects (see Resources > Rubrics) |

## Project Path

### Intro (~10 min, Day 1)

- Review the poster brief and the required-skills checklist: justified typeface pairing,
  refined Character panel settings, one special-type element, clean text flow, and outlined final
  type.

### Concept & Typeface Selection (~50 min, Day 1)

1. Choose a poster topic — a quote, a single word, an event, or a message you want to communicate.
2. Select a typeface pairing (usually one display/headline typeface plus one more restrained
   body/support typeface) and be ready to justify the choice using 5.1's classification
   vocabulary.
3. Begin a rough composition sketch or digital layout.

{% include organizedesign.md %}

### Develop (remaining project days)

- Build out your poster in Illustrator: refine kerning, tracking, and leading (5.2); incorporate
  at least one special-type element such as Type on a Path or a shaped area type block (5.3);
  keep any longer text threaded cleanly with no overflow (5.4).

**Structured Peer Review.** Once your poster has a legible draft composition, do a full Plus/Delta
peer review pass with a partner, focused specifically on legibility and typeface justification:

{% include peerreview.md %}

### Implement (final project day)

- Finalize your composition and proofread all wording carefully — **once your wording is
  completely final**, convert your finished type to outlines (5.4).
- Write your **typeface-justification statement** — a short explanation of why you chose your
  typeface pairing and how your Character panel choices support your poster's message.
- Export your finished poster as a PNG or JPG for your portfolio.

{% include portfolio.md %}

### Reflect (final project day)

{: .reflection }
How did your typeface choice and Character panel settings work together to support your poster's
message? What would you change if you had one more class period?
