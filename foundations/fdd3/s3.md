# S | 3 | Vector Self-Portrait

You've spent this unit learning color theory, shape tools, swatches and gradients, and precise
composition. Today, you'll combine all four into one project: a self-portrait built entirely from
shapes, deliberately colored, and carefully arranged.

## Project Briefing (~10 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="self-portrait-planning-sheet" title="Vector Self-Portrait Planning Sheet" presentation="placeholder" %}

Watch the teacher build one simple facial feature (an eye, made from 2–3 shapes) from start to
finish: block it out with shape primitives, apply a global swatch color, then use the Align panel
to center it precisely — the exact workflow you'll repeat across your whole portrait.

## Reference Photo

If you haven't already, take a medium close-up or close-up photo of yourself to work from.
Consider as you take it:

- What strong identifiers (age, attire, cultural connections, mood/expression) do you want to
  emphasize?
- What is being expressed by your pose (frontal, profile, or partly turned)? Where are you
  looking?
- What color scheme, from 3.1, will you use — and what mood does it communicate?

## Project Work Time (~23 min)

1. **Plan first:** fill out your planning sheet — identifiers, a shape inventory for each major
   feature, your chosen color scheme, and a quick composition sketch — before opening Illustrator.
2. **Block out the main forms** using shape primitives from 3.2 (Rectangle, Ellipse, Polygon,
   Star, Line Segment) — no Pen tool, no freehand drawing. Build the face shape, then layer in
   features.
3. **Add middle- and background-ground detail.** Background imagery doesn't have to literally
   match your original photo's background — consider repeating, copying, and flipping shapes to
   build texture.
4. **Apply your color scheme (3.1, 3.3):** save at least one color as a global swatch, and use at
   least one gradient (linear or radial) somewhere in the piece — a good candidate is the
   background or a shaded area.
5. **Align, distribute, and arrange (3.4):** use the Align panel and a Key Object to precisely
   position paired features (eyes, ears), fix stacking order so nothing important is hidden
   behind something else, then group the finished composition.
6. **Gallery-walk check-in:** partners look at 2–3 classmates' in-progress portraits and try to
   name the color scheme used, before asking.
