# 11.3 | Packaging & Promotional Design Concepts

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-packaging-promo.md %}

Every product you've ever bought was designed twice — once as the physical package it ships in,
and once as the promotional pieces (flyers, posters, ads) that convinced you to buy it in the
first place. Today's lesson is about how those two pieces work together as one brand system —
and it's where you'll pick the brand you'll design for through the rest of this unit.

## Bell Ringer (~10 min)

**F1 Vocab Warm-Up.** Answer 5 quick application questions using today's vocabulary — for
example: *"A client wants their box to fold shut without tape or glue — what document tells the
manufacturer exactly where to cut and fold?"*

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

Guided notes: a **dieline** is the technical blueprint for how a package is cut, scored, and
folded — the 2D flat pattern a designer's artwork gets placed onto before it becomes a real 3D
package. A brand's **identity** (logo, color palette, typography, imagery style) has to stay
consistent everywhere it appears, which is why **brand guidelines** exist — a reference document
so every designer who touches the brand produces work that still looks like the same brand.
**Promotional collateral** (flyers, posters, table tents, social ads) supports the primary
packaging, usually built around a **tagline** and a clear **call to action**.

{: .discussion }
Think of a brand you recognize instantly. What's the ONE visual element that gives it away, even
with no logo or words visible?

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

{: .highlight }
**Paired annotation.** With a partner, look at the provided packaging piece and its matching
promotional flyer — both for the same fictional brand. Circle and label every place the SAME
brand element (logo, color, tagline, typography) shows up in both pieces. Compare your findings
with another pair.

## Studio/Project Work Time (~30 min)

This is where **your** brand starts. In your sketchbook or on scratch paper:

1. Choose a product and a brand name — real (something you'd actually want to design for), or
   fully invented. This is the brand you'll design a promotional spread AND a packaging mockup
   for in 11.4 and S11.
2. Sketch 3–5 quick **logo mark** ideas.
3. Sketch a rough **product label** layout.
4. Sketch a simple **dieline** shape for your product's package (a box, a bag, a bottle label —
   keep the structure simple).

{: .reflection }
Which brand element (logo, color, tagline, or type) was easiest to spot repeating across the
provided example's two pieces, and which was hardest?
