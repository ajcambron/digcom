# 11.2 | Working with Grids & Multi-Page Documents

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-grids-multipage.md %}

A single page is one thing — but a yearbook, a magazine, or a brochure needs many pages that all
feel like part of the *same* publication. Today you'll learn how Illustrator handles that, and
where its limits are.

## Bell Ringer (~10 min)

From memory, list as many of 11.1's page-layout terms (margin, gutter, column, hierarchy,
alignment, spread, folio) as you can in 90 seconds. Check your list against the vocab bank above.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

Illustrator isn't dedicated page-layout software — it doesn't auto-flow text between pages or
maintain true master pages the way InDesign does. What it *does* have is **multiple artboards**
in one document, which we can use as a lightweight stand-in for "pages," as long as we rebuild
consistency by hand every time.

Watch as your teacher adds a second artboard to a document and manually copies over the grid
guides and a repeating **folio** element (page number + running header) — noting every step that
would be automatic in real page-layout software but isn't here.

{: .discussion }
Why might a real publishing studio still choose dedicated page-layout software over Illustrator
for a 100-page yearbook, even though Illustrator can technically hold multiple artboards?

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

As a class, set up **artboard 1**: a grid (from 11.1), consistent margins, and a folio in the
same position every time. Then, with a partner, replicate that exact setup onto **artboard 2** —
compare your result against another pair's before moving on.

## Studio/Project Work Time (~30 min)

Independently, build out a **4-artboard document** (think of it as 4 pages of a small
publication): every artboard should share the same grid, the same margins, and a folio in a
consistent position. This is the multi-page document you'll build your yearbook spread inside of
next lesson.

{: .reflection }
What got harder, or easier, once the same layout needed to repeat across multiple pages instead
of just one?

## Exit Ticket / Wrap-Up (~5 min)

Save your file, then take a screenshot showing all 4 artboards.
