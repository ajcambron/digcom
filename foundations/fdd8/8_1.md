# 8.1 | Brushes & Pattern Making

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-brushes-patterns.md %}

> A brush turns a boring path into a textured line without you drawing every bristle by hand —
> and a pattern turns one small tile into wallpaper without you drawing it twice.

Unit 7 was about controlling how *one* object looks. Unit 8 is about multiplying that look
across a whole surface — brushed paths, repeating patterns, reusable symbols, traced artwork, and
even AI-generated starting points — building toward a sticker pack or merch pattern project.

## [10 Mins] Bell Ringer / Hook

{: .discussion }
Look at these two identical squiggly lines — one is plain, one looks like it was painted with a
loaded brush. What do you think changed between them?

> "Nothing about the path itself changed — only the brush applied to it. Same idea as
> yesterday's... wait, today's Appearance panel work, just for strokes made of texture instead of
> flat color."

## [10 Mins] Direct Instruction

**"I Do":** the teacher live-demos, on a hand-drawn squiggle path:

1. Opening the Brushes panel and applying an **Art brush**, then swapping it for a **Scatter
   brush**, comparing how each stretches vs. scatters along the path.
2. Opening the **Stroke** options to show brush weight is still just stroke weight underneath.
3. Switching to a small icon shape and choosing **Object > Pattern > Make** to enter **Generate
   Pattern** mode, live-tiling the icon across the canvas.
4. Adjusting the pattern's tile type, spacing, and overlap while the live preview updates, then
   clicking Done to save it as a swatch in the Swatches panel.
5. Filling a large rectangle with the new pattern swatch to show the finished repeating result.

{: .highlight }
**Check for Understanding**
**Recall:** "What panel do you open to apply a brush to a path?"
**Analysis:** "Why did the Scatter brush look different from the Art brush on the exact same
path?"
**Synthesis:** "You're designing wrapping paper. Would you reach for a brush, a pattern, or both?
Explain your choice."

## [23 Mins] Guided Practice

{% include worksheet.html file="brushes-patterns-reference" title="Brushes & Patterns Reference" presentation="placeholder" %}

**Turn and Talk (2 min):** compare with your partner which brush type you're picking for your
practice path and why.

1. Draw a simple path (a line, wave, or loop) and apply a brush of your choice — try at least 2
   brush types before settling on one.
2. Draw or reuse a small shape (an icon, star, or simple object) as your pattern's building
   block.
3. Use **Object > Pattern > Make** to build a repeating pattern from it, adjusting spacing until
   there's no obvious hard seam where tiles meet.
4. Save the pattern and apply it as a fill to a large shape to preview the full repeat.
