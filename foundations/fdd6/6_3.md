You already know how to build area type from Unit 5. Today's about the paragraph-level polish
that turns "text that fits in a box" into "text that's genuinely comfortable to read."

## Direct Instruction — Live Demo (~10 min)

> "See these gaps stretched out between words? That's justified alignment fighting a column
> that's too narrow for it — let's fix it."

The teacher live-demos on a sample area-type paragraph:

1. **Alignment** — comparing left, center, right, and justified.
2. **Indents** — left, right, and first-line.
3. **Space before/after** — adding breathing room between paragraphs.
4. **Hyphenation** — toggling it on and off to compare the effect on a narrow column.

{: .discussion }
**Recall:** What's the risk of using justified alignment in a narrow column? **Analysis:** Why
does adding space after a paragraph usually read better than adding an extra manual line break?
**Synthesis:** If a client's brochure column looked "too dense," which 2 settings from today would
you try first, and why?

## Guided Practice — Revise a Paragraph (~23 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="fdd6-area-type-reference" title="Paragraph Settings & Area Type" presentation="placeholder" %}

1. **Diagnose (~5 min):** open the provided file and identify what's making the paragraph hard to
   read (awkward justification gaps, no space between paragraphs, etc.).
2. **Revise (~18 min):** adjust alignment, indents, space before/after, and hyphenation to resolve
   the issues. Screenshot your before and after for comparison.

{: .highlight }
There's no single "correct" paragraph setting for every situation — the goal is readability. Try
more than one option and trust your eye.
