# 6.2 | Digital Color: Modes & Gamuts

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-color-gamut.md %}

You already know CMYK, RGB, and HSB from Unit 3. Today asks a harder question: even within the
right color mode, why do some colors that look perfect on your screen come out disappointing when
printed?

## Direct Instruction — Live Demo (~10 min)

> "Watch this warning triangle. It's Illustrator telling me this exact color can't actually be
> printed — no printer's ink can make it."

The teacher live-demos: selecting a bright neon color, pointing out the small out-of-gamut warning
triangle in the Color panel, then clicking it to see Illustrator's nearest printable equivalent.

{: .discussion }
**Recall:** What does the out-of-gamut warning triangle mean? **Analysis:** Why can a screen (RGB)
display colors a printer (CMYK) simply cannot reproduce? **Synthesis:** If a client insisted on
keeping their exact neon-green brand color for a printed poster, what would you tell them about
their options?

## Guided Practice — Fix-This-File (~23 min)

{% include worksheet.html file="fdd6-color-gamut-notes" title="6.2 Guided Notes: Digital Color: Modes & Gamuts" presentation="placeholder" %}

1. **Guided notes (~8 min):** complete the fill-in-the-blank notes and label the RGB/CMYK diagram.
2. **Fix-This-File (~15 min):** open the provided file — it has **3 out-of-gamut colors** intended
   for a printed piece. Use the Color panel's warning indicator to find and correct each to its
   nearest in-gamut equivalent.

{: .highlight }
Clicking directly on the small warning swatch next to the triangle snaps a color to its closest
in-gamut equivalent automatically — but always eyeball the result to make sure it still fits your
design.
