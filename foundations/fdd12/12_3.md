# 12.3 | Portfolio Curation

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-portfolio-presentation.md %}

You've made a lot of work this year — comps, artboards, a self-portrait, a stained-glass piece,
a poster, a sticker pack, a brand package, and more. Today's job is turning all of that into one
coherent portfolio that actually represents you as a designer.

## Bell Ringer (~10 min)

Without looking anything up yet, list every FDD project you can remember making this year, oldest
to newest. Then check your Google Drive folders and Google Sites Portfolio — how close was your
memory?

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

Guided notes: a strong portfolio isn't "everything I've ever made" — it's **curated**. Look for
**range** (different skills, not five versions of the same thing), your genuinely **best work**
(not just your most recent), and pieces **relevant** to whatever you want the portfolio to say
about you. You can organize **chronologically** (showing growth over the year) or **thematically**
(grouped by skill or project type) — either is valid, but pick one on purpose. Every piece needs
a short **project description**: what was the goal, what was your process, what tools did you
use?

{: .discussion }
Would you rather a portfolio show your growth over time, or only your best final work with no
weaker early pieces visible? Why?

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

As a class, critique a sample portfolio page together — what's missing, what works well. Then,
with a partner, draft one practice project description together before working independently.

## Studio/Project Work Time (~30 min)

{% include portfolio.md %}

1. Review your own year of FDD work. List every project.
2. Select and **rank** your strongest pieces — aim for range, not just quantity.
3. Draft a project description for at least 3 of your selected pieces.

{: .reflection }
Which piece almost didn't make the cut, and why did you include it anyway (or leave it out)?
