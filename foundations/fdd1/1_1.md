# 1.1 | What is Design?

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-what-is-design.md %}

> "Design is not just what it looks like and feels like. Design is how it works." —Steve Jobs

No computers today — this entire 78-minute period runs on paper, split into guided notes and a
hands-on handout.

## Part 1 — Guided Notes (~35 min)

Copy the outline below onto your notes handout, filling in each blank as we go through it
together.

1. Graphic design surrounds us: it is the ______ we read, the ______ we buy, and the ______ we
   inhabit. (Hint: think about what you saw on the way to school this morning.)
2. Graphic designers try to make every aspect of our lives defined and ______ using a language
   of type and imagery.
3. Design is a ______ art form — we're exposed to thousands of examples of it every single day.
4. Design is not decoration, it is ______ ______. Having design software doesn't automatically
   make someone good at design, any more than having a camera makes someone a photographer.
5. Richard Grefé, a former Executive Director of AIGA (the professional association for design),
   described design as "the intermediary between ______ and ______." Properly applied design can
   influence how people behave, think, and even shape public policy.
6. List at least 6 careers in graphic design from the list your teacher shares (e.g. advertising
   design, book design, brand & identity design, environmental design, illustration, information
   design, motion design, package design, publication design, wayfinding design...).

{: .discussion }
Guiding Questions — be ready to discuss as a class: *Where and how do we encounter images in our
world? What is graphic design? How do designers grow and become accomplished?*

## Part 2 — Handout: Design All Around You (~35 min)

On your Design Spotting handout:

1. List **6 examples of design** you have personally encountered in the last 24 hours (a cereal
   box, a road sign, an app icon, a poster in the hallway, packaging, a book cover — anything).
2. For each example, name which **design career** from Part 1's list it belongs to.
3. Choose your **2 strongest examples** and, in a few sentences each, explain: what message was
   this design trying to communicate, and who do you think its audience was?
4. Sketch a **quick thumbnail** (small, rough pencil sketch — you'll learn this term formally in
   1.2) of one of your two examples, just to start getting comfortable putting pencil to paper.

{: .reflection }
Before today, would you have called any of your 6 examples "design"? What changed your mind, or
confirmed what you already thought?

📤 **Exit Ticket:** turn in your completed guided notes *and* your Design Spotting handout
(6 examples, career tags, 2 written breakdowns, 1 thumbnail sketch).
