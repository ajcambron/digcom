## Part 2 — Line Assignment (~50 min)

Designers rarely use their first idea — they generate many quickly, evaluate them, and refine
their best one. You'll use that exact process (**thumbnail → rough → comp**) for the element of
**line**.

{% include worksheet.html file="thumbnail-sheet" title="Thumbnail Sheet (16 boxes)" %}
{% include worksheet.html file="roughs-sheet" title="Roughs Sheet (3 boxes)" %}

1. **Thumbnails (~12 min):** on your thumbnail sheet, draw **16 small thumbnail sketches**,
   using nothing but line — vary the thickness, direction, and spacing in each box. Experiment
   with as many different types of line as you can think of: thick, thin, wavy, dotted. Work
   fast; the goal is quantity of ideas, not a finished piece in every box.
2. **Select (~2 min):** look over your 16 thumbnails and choose your **3 strongest**. A strong
   idea might have interesting composition, movement, or contrast — there isn't just one right
   answer.
3. **Roughs (~15 min):** develop each of your 3 chosen thumbnails into a **rough sketch** —
   slower and more careful than a thumbnail, still in pencil.
4. **Select (~1 min):** choose the single rough you think is strongest. Ask a neighbor if you're
   not sure — a second opinion helps.
5. **Comp (~20 min):** on a clean sheet of paper, develop your chosen rough into a final
   **comprehensive design ("comp")** — start in pencil, finish in black marker. This is the
   neatly-executed, best version of your idea.

{: .highlight }
Placing a clean sheet of paper between your hand and your work as you finish in marker keeps it
from smudging.

## Wrap-Up (~6 min)

Photograph your finished comp — you'll need this photo as reference on the S | 1 project day,
when you'll try to recreate your strongest comp from the whole unit in Illustrator.

{: .reflection }
What makes your comp different from your first thumbnail? What changed between the two?
