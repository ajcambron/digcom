# 1.4 | Communicating Design Plans

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-design-plans.md %}

No computers today — guided notes, then a full plan-and-pitch on paper.

## Part 1 — Guided Notes (~30 min)

Copy the outline below onto your notes handout.

1. Designers don't just make things — they communicate the **______** for it first, so the
   client can react before time is spent on a finished piece.
2. Recall from 1.2: a **rough sketch** is often shown to a **______** for approval — this is one
   of the earliest, most common ways a design plan actually gets communicated.
3. The repeating cycle of sharing work, getting feedback, and revising in response to it is
   called the **______** ______.
4. A **______** ______ is a reference document that defines a design's consistent visual rules —
   colors, fonts, spacing, logo use.
5. A **______** ______ is a written document laying out a project's goals, audience,
   requirements, and constraints before work begins. (This connects directly to 1.3's client
   request — a project brief formalizes what you found there.)
6. Put these design-process steps in order: Draft, Sketch, Final, Feedback, Revise.
   (Trick: there's more than one correct order, depending on how many feedback loops happen — be
   ready to explain yours.)

{: .discussion }
Why might it be a bad idea to build a finished design before showing anyone your plan for it?

## Part 2 — Handout: Plan & Pitch (~40 min)

1. **Sketch a plan (15 min):** for a made-up project (e.g., a poster for a school club), sketch a
   one-page plan that includes: a thumbnail sketch of the layout, one labeled color choice, and
   one sentence describing the target audience. This is a plan, not a finished design — it
   should look rough.
2. **Write a mini style note (10 min):** below your sketch, write 2–3 rules a finished version
   would follow (e.g., "logo always appears top-left," "only these two fonts," "background is
   always the dark color") — a miniature style guide.
3. **Pitch it (15 min):** in groups of 3, each student presents their plan out loud in under a
   minute, as if the other two are the client. The "client" group gives one piece of feedback
   using the feedback-loop language from Part 1 (e.g., "I'd revise ___ before I approved this").

{: .reflection }
If you handed your one-page plan to a real client, what's one question you'd expect them to ask
you back? Did your practice pitch group actually ask something like that?

📤 **Exit Ticket:** turn in your guided notes, your one-page plan (thumbnail + color choice +
audience sentence + mini style note), and the one piece of feedback your pitch group gave you.
