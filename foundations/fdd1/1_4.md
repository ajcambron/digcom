No computers today — guided notes, the final element assignment, then a class closure activity.

## Part 1 — Guided Notes: Communicating Design Plans (~10 min)

Copy the outline below onto your notes handout.

{% include worksheet.html file="guided-notes-1-4" title="1.4 Guided Notes: Communicating Design Plans" %}

1. Designers don't just make things — they communicate the **______** for it first, so a client
   can react before time is spent on a finished piece. Recall from 1.1: a **rough sketch** is
   often shown to a **______** for approval — this is one of the earliest, most common ways a
   plan actually gets communicated.
2. The repeating cycle of sharing work, getting feedback, and revising is the **______** ______.
3. A **______** ______ is a written document laying out a project's goals, audience,
   requirements, and constraints before work begins — connecting directly to 1.3's client/
   audience/goals.

{: .discussion }
Why might it be a bad idea to build a finished design before showing anyone your plan for it?

## Part 2 — Color Assignment (~42 min)

Last element, same process — thumbnail → rough → comp — this time using **color**.

{% include worksheet.html file="thumbnail-sheet" title="Thumbnail Sheet (16 boxes)" %}

1. **Thumbnails (~12 min):** using colored pencil, draw **16 thumbnails** of abstract patterns.
   Draw on what you noticed in the Line, Shape, and Texture assignments. Apply two contrasting
   colors of the purest hue (e.g. purple vs. violet-red, green vs. yellow-green) — consider what
   happens where they overlap, or if you leave an area white.
2. **Select (~2 min):** choose your **3 strongest** thumbnails.
3. **Roughs (~14 min):** develop each into a rough, using different hues of your chosen colors
   to create a sense of motion/energy or calm/stillness.
4. **Select (~1 min):** choose your single strongest rough.
5. **Comp (~13 min):** develop it into a finished comp using your full set of colored pencils.

## Part 3 — Class Closure (~10 min)

Put all four of your finished comps (Line, Shape, Texture, Color) on the wall or your desk
together and look at them as a set.

1. Do your four comps share a unifying visual idea, or do they look like unrelated pieces?
2. If you repeated all four assignments, what would you do differently to unify them?

## Wrap-Up (~6 min)

Photograph your finished Color comp. You now have all 4 element comps — decide which one is your
overall strongest; you'll recreate it in Illustrator on the S | 1 project day.

{: .reflection }
Which of your 4 comps (Line, Shape, Texture, or Color) is your strongest overall, and why?

📥 **Turn In:** your guided notes, your 16 thumbnails, your 3 roughs, your finished comp, and
your answer to which of your 4 comps is strongest.
