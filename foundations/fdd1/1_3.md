# 1.3 | Purpose, Audience & Client Goals

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-audience-goals.md %}

No computers today — guided notes, then two rounds of client-request analysis on paper.

## Part 1 — Guided Notes (~30 min)

Copy the outline below onto your notes handout.

1. Every design project has a **______** (who is asking for the work) and a **______**
   ______ (who the work is actually for) — and they aren't always the same person.
2. A designer's first job before making anything is figuring out the client's real
   ______ and who the audience really is.
3. **Demographics** are ______________________________ used to describe an audience.
4. **Accessibility** means designing so people with a range of ______ (vision, hearing, motor,
   cognitive) can use and understand the work.
5. A project's **______** ______ is its defined boundaries — what it will and won't include.
   When those boundaries keep growing after the project has already started, that's called
   **______** ______, and it's one of the most common ways real design projects go over
   budget or past deadline.
6. A project's **______** ______ is the schedule of milestones and deadlines it's expected to
   follow.

{: .discussion }
Think of a poster, ad, or app you've seen recently that clearly *wasn't* made for you. How could
you tell?

## Part 2 — Handout: Client Request Breakdown (~20 min)

1. Read the sample client request on your handout.
2. On your own, identify: who is the client, who is the target audience, what is the stated
   goal, and one accessibility consideration the client didn't think to mention but should have.
3. As a class, compare answers — there's often more than one reasonable read on "audience."

## Part 3 — Handout: Write Your Own (~20 min)

1. Write your own one-paragraph client request for an imaginary business or organization,
   including a client, an audience, and a goal.
2. **Constraint:** your request must include one detail that seems to contradict the stated
   audience — your partner has to catch it. (Example: "for our teen audience" but the imagery
   description sounds like it's for toddlers.)
3. Trade with a partner. Identify their client, audience, goal, one accessibility consideration,
   and the contradictory detail.

{: .reflection }
When you read your partner's client request, was anything unclear about who the audience was
supposed to be? What would you ask the "client" to clarify?

📤 **Exit Ticket:** turn in your guided notes, the sample-request breakdown, your own client
request, and your partner's breakdown of it (including the contradiction they caught).
