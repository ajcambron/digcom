# 1.3 | Purpose, Audience & Client Goals

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-audience-goals.md %}

## Every design has a client and an audience
Even when you're the one making the decisions, every design project has a **client** (who is
asking for the work) and a **target audience** (who the work is actually for) — and they aren't
always the same person. A designer's first job is figuring out what the client actually needs,
and who the audience really is, before making anything.

{: .discussion }
Think of a poster, ad, or app you've seen recently that clearly *wasn't* made for you. How could
you tell?

## On Paper Today

1. **Warm-up (5 min):** read the short sample client request on your handout. On paper, write
   down: who is the client, and who is the target audience? (They're different in this example.)
2. **Guided practice:** as a class, break down the sample client request together — identify the
   goal, the audience's demographics, and one accessibility consideration the client didn't
   think to mention but should have.
3. **Independent practice:** on your worksheet, write your own one-paragraph client request for
   an imaginary business, then trade with a partner and have them identify your client, audience,
   goal, and one accessibility consideration — all on paper.

{: .reflection }
When you read your partner's client request, was anything unclear about who the audience was
supposed to be? What would you ask the "client" to clarify?

📤 **Exit Ticket:** turn in your worksheet with your own client request *and* your partner's
breakdown of it.
