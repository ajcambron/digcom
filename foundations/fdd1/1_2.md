## Part 1 — Guided Notes (~35 min)

Copy the outline below onto your notes handout.

1. The **elements of design** are the raw building blocks every design is made of: ______,
   ______, ______, ______, and ______ (fill in from the vocabulary above).
2. The **principles of design** are the rules for *arranging* those building blocks:
   ______, ______, ______, ______, and ______.
3. One of the hardest things for new designers to accept: design is rarely
   **representational** (a realistic depiction of a real object) — it's usually **abstract** (an
   idea without a concrete, literal form). An abstract image can be read as more than one thing
   at once — that's called **ambiguity**, and it's a *feature*, not a mistake.
4. Designers rarely use their first idea. They generate many ideas quickly as small
   ______ ______ (thumbnails), pick their strongest ones, and develop those into more
   polished ______ ______ (roughs) — often shown to a client for approval — before finishing a
   single neatly-executed ______ ______, or "______" for short.
5. A design's **format** is ______________________________________ (define it in your own
   words, using the vocabulary above).

{: .discussion }
Why would a designer want to generate 16 different ideas instead of just picking their first one
and polishing it?

## Part 2 — Handout: Thumbnail Warm-Up (~35 min)

This is a practice round for the process you'll use on the S | 1 project — small-scale, so you
get comfortable with it before it counts.

1. On your thumbnail warm-up sheet, draw **6 thumbnail sketches** using nothing but **line** —
   vary the thickness, direction, and spacing in each box. Don't think too hard about any single
   box; the goal is speed and quantity.
2. Look back over your 6 thumbnails and circle your **2 strongest** — the ones with the most
   interesting composition or contrast.
3. Pick your favorite of the 2 and develop it into **one rough sketch** — slower, more careful,
   still in pencil.
4. On your finished rough, label one **element** (e.g., "line — thick vs. thin") and one
   **principle** (e.g., "repetition — the diagonal lines repeat across the page") that you can
   point to in your own work.

{: .reflection }
Which of your 6 thumbnails surprised you the most — one you didn't expect to like as much as you
did?

📤 **Exit Ticket:** turn in your guided notes, your 6 thumbnails, and your 1 developed rough,
with at least one element and one principle labeled.
