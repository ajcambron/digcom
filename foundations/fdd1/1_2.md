# 1.2 | Elements & Principles of Design

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-elements-principles.md %}

## Basic Elements of Design
The raw building blocks every design is made of — line, shape, color/value, texture, and
mass/form. See the vocabulary above for definitions of each.

## Principles of Design
The rules for *arranging* those building blocks — balance, alignment, contrast, unity/harmony,
and proportion.

## Design Layout Principles
Principles specifically about arranging elements on a page or screen — proximity, alignment,
repetition, contrast, the rule of thirds, and the rule of Phi/Golden Ratio.

{: .discussion }
Pick any principle above (balance, contrast, proximity, rule of thirds — any of them). Find one
example of it somewhere in this room or on your own clothing right now.

## On Paper Today

1. **Warm-up (5 min):** on the handout, sort a list of 10 words into two columns — "Element" or
   "Principle." (No computers — this is a pencil-and-paper sort.)
2. **Guided practice:** as a class, look at a printed example design (handout). Annotate it by
   hand: circle one place where an *element* is clearly used, and draw an arrow to one place
   where a *principle* is clearly at work.
3. **Independent practice:** on your own printed image, label at least 2 elements and 2
   principles you can identify, writing directly on the page.

{: .reflection }
Which was easier to spot in your image — an element or a principle? Why do you think that is?

📤 **Exit Ticket:** turn in your annotated image with at least 2 elements and 2 principles
labeled.
