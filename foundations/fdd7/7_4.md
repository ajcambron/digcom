# 7.4 | Opacity, Blending & Masks

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-transparency-masks.md %}

> A clipping mask asks "inside the shape, or outside?" — nothing in between. An opacity mask asks
> "how much?" — and can answer with a gradient. Today you'll use both, on purpose.

This closes out Unit 7's look at appearance. You've organized objects (7.1), stacked their fills
and strokes (7.2), and added effects (7.3) — today's opacity, blending, and masking tools control
how all of that interacts with whatever sits underneath it, which is exactly what you'll need for
this unit's album cover / gig poster project.

## [10 Mins] Bell Ringer / Hook

{: .discussion }
Here are two versions of the same photo faded into a background: one has a hard, clean edge; one
fades out gradually at the top. Which technique — a shape, or a gradient — do you think made
each one?

> "Both are masks. One's a clipping mask, one's an opacity mask, and knowing which tool makes
> which effect is today's whole lesson."

## [10 Mins] Direct Instruction

**"I Do":** using a photo placed behind a logo mark, the teacher live-demos:

1. Selecting the logo mark and lowering its **Opacity** in the Transparency panel, then changing
   its **Blending Mode** from Normal to Multiply and then Screen, comparing the results live.
2. Drawing a shape over the photo and using **Object > Clipping Mask > Make** to crop the photo
   to that shape's hard edge — then **Release** to undo it.
3. Rebuilding the same crop as an **opacity mask** instead: duplicating the shape, filling it
   with a black-to-white gradient, and dragging it onto the mask thumbnail in the Transparency
   panel — pointing out the edge now fades instead of cutting off sharply.
4. Comparing the two results side by side and naming the difference: a clipping mask is a hard
   yes/no; an opacity mask can partially show through.

{: .highlight }
**Check for Understanding**
**Recall:** "What two things does the Transparency panel let you control on a selected object?"
**Analysis:** "Why did the gradient-based mask fade gradually, while the shape-based clipping
mask cut off sharply?"
**Synthesis:** "You want text to fade smoothly into a photo background, like it's disappearing
into fog. Which mask type would you use, and what would you fill it with?"

## [23 Mins] Guided Practice

{% include worksheet.html file="transparency-masks-reference" title="Transparency & Masks Reference" presentation="placeholder" %}

1. Place the provided background texture behind your saved 7.3 logo mark.
2. Set your logo mark to a blending mode other than Normal and note how it changes against the
   texture.
3. Build a **clipping mask** cropping the texture to a shape of your choice.
4. Rebuild the same idea as an **opacity mask** using a black-to-white gradient, so the texture
   fades instead of cutting off.
5. In the last 5 minutes, leave your screen up for a **quick gallery walk** — look at one other
   group's masks and note one thing you'd borrow for your own S7 project.
