# 7.1 | The Layers Panel

## Key Terms

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-layers-panel.md %}

> A messy Layers panel is a messy file — if you can't find an object by its name, neither can
> your client, and neither will you in three weeks.

Every file you've built so far in Illustrator has lived on a single, unnamed layer. That's fine
for a five-object composition — it falls apart the moment a file has 40 objects and three
deadlines. Over Unit 7 you'll learn to control **where objects sit** (the Layers panel, today),
**how they look** (Appearance, Effects, and Transparency — 7.2–7.4), building toward a finished
poster in this unit's project.

## [10 Mins] Bell Ringer / Hook

{: .discussion }
Put these five things in order from "you'd touch it first" to "you'd touch it last": a folder
inside a filing cabinet drawer, the filing cabinet itself, a sticky note on top of the folder,
the drawer, the room the cabinet is in.

> "Whatever's true for that filing cabinet is true for Illustrator's Layers panel — what's listed
> higher in the panel sits visually **in front of** everything listed below it. Today we're
> learning to control that stack on purpose instead of by accident."

## [10 Mins] Direct Instruction

**"I Do":** using `unit7_layers_start.ai` (an intentionally messy 6-object file — a sky, two
clouds, a mountain, and two birds, all dumped on "Layer 1"), the teacher live-demos:

1. Opening the Layers panel (Window > Layers) and noting every object already has its own
   **sublayer**, just unnamed.
2. Double-clicking a sublayer's name to rename it something useful (`Sky`, `Mountain`, `Cloud —
   left`).
3. Creating three new top-level **layers** (`Background`, `Midground`, `Foreground`) and dragging
   the renamed sublayers into the correct one.
4. Dragging a whole layer up or down in the panel to fix a **stacking order** mistake (a bird
   accidentally hidden behind the mountain).
5. Selecting the two cloud sublayers and choosing **Merge Selected** from the panel menu — noting
   this keeps their relative order, unlike Flatten Artwork, which would collapse the whole file.

{: .highlight }
**Check for Understanding** — cold-call three students, one question each:
**Recall:** "What does dragging a layer to the top of the list do to that object?"
**Analysis:** "Why did merging the two clouds not change how the image looks, even though the
Layers panel changed?"
**Synthesis:** "If you were handed a 40-object file on one layer, what would your first three
layer names be, and why those three?"

## [23 Mins] Guided Practice

{% include worksheet.html file="layers-panel-reference" title="Layers Panel Reference" presentation="placeholder" %}

**Turn and Talk (2 min):** before opening your file, tell your partner: what 3 layer names would
*you* use for `unit7_layers_start.ai`, and in what order?

1. Open your own copy of `unit7_layers_start.ai`.
2. Rename every sublayer to something specific — no object should be left as "Path" or
   "<Group>."
3. Create at least 3 named layers and sort every object into one.
4. Find and fix the stacking-order mistake (a bird is hidden — find it and move its layer above
   the object hiding it).
5. Select two related sublayers and use **Merge Selected** to combine them into one.
6. Lock your `Background` layer so it can't be accidentally edited, and take a screenshot of your
   finished Layers panel.
