# 7.2 | Appearance: Fill & Stroke

{: .vocab }
{% include vocab/fdd-appearance-panel.md %}

> An object in Illustrator isn't just "a shape with a color" — it's a whole stack of fills and
> strokes, and the Appearance panel is where you see and control that stack.

Yesterday you organized *where* objects sit. Today you'll dig into *how one single object* can
carry more visual complexity than it looks like it should — multiple fills, multiple strokes,
stacked and reordered, all without adding a single extra path.

## [10 Mins] Bell Ringer / Hook

{: .discussion }
Look at this circle: it has a thick colored ring, a thin white ring inside that, and a solid fill
in the middle. How many separate *objects* do you think that took to draw?

> "Trick question — that's one circle, carrying three attributes. Today's tool for seeing and
> controlling that is the Appearance panel."

## [10 Mins] Direct Instruction

**"I Do":** starting from a single plain circle, the teacher live-demos:

1. Opening the Appearance panel (Window > Appearance) and pointing out its default single fill +
   single stroke.
2. Clicking **Add New Stroke** twice to add two more strokes, each a different weight and color,
   building a "target" ring effect.
3. Dragging one stroke above another in the panel to change which ring shows in front.
4. Using **Duplicate Item** on the fill, then changing the duplicate's blending mode to add a
   subtle inner tint.
5. Sampling the finished look onto a second, unrelated circle with the **Eyedropper tool** —
   one click, whole appearance copied.
6. Selecting both circles and opening **Recolor Artwork** to shift the whole palette to a
   different hue in one move.

{: .highlight }
**Check for Understanding**
**Recall:** "What panel shows every fill and stroke on a selected object, in order?"
**Analysis:** "Why did dragging a stroke to a new position in the panel change what the object
looks like, even though I didn't touch the artwork itself?"
**Synthesis:** "If a client asked for the same logo mark in three color variations, which tool
from today would get you there fastest, and why?"

## [23 Mins] Guided Practice

{% include worksheet.html file="appearance-panel-reference" title="Appearance Panel Reference" presentation="placeholder" %}

1. Draw a simple base shape (circle, hexagon, or square) for your own logo mark.
2. Build up at least **two strokes and one fill** in the Appearance panel, reordering at least
   once to get the visual result you want.
3. Duplicate one attribute and adjust it (a different weight, opacity, or blending mode) to add
   depth.
4. **Trade computers with a partner (10 min):** use the Eyedropper to sample your partner's
   finished appearance onto a shape of your own. Discuss: what looked different once it was on
   your shape instead of theirs?
5. Select your own mark and use **Recolor Artwork** to generate one alternate color version.

## [30 Mins] GMetrix / BrainBuffet Self-Guided Strand

Continue **GMetrix Module 4 — Dobson Poster**. Notice how many separate fill/stroke attributes
the poster's title treatment uses — that's the same Appearance-panel stacking you just practiced,
just applied to type instead of a shape.
