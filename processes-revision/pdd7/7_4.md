# 7.4 | Generative AI Tools & the Ethics Line

{: .highlight }
**NEW lesson (stub).** No existing PDD lesson covers this. Written for the draft revision; materials marked "Stub" in the teacher notes still need to be built.

Photoshop can now make pixels from a text prompt. The exam tests it, and competitions ban it. Today you'll learn both sides.

{% include lesson-parts/stinger.md %}

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

**Generative Fill** creates new content in a selection from a text **prompt**; **Generative Expand** extends the canvas. **Remove Background** masks the subject in one click. **Sky Replacement** swaps a sky and matches the light. Adobe attaches **Content Credentials** to show AI was used. TSA's honor statement forbids generative AI in portfolio images.

> "Same selection, two tools. Content-Aware Fill used my pixels. Generative Fill invented new ones. That difference is the whole ethics question."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What does Generative Fill need from you?"

> **Analysis:** "How is Generative Fill different from Content-Aware Fill?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Should photo contests allow AI? Defend a position with one rule."

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Try** Generative Fill, Remove Background, and Sky Replacement on a practice image.
2. **Fill in** the 'where it's allowed' chart.
3. **Four corners:** stand by your answer to "if AI makes part of a photo, is it still your photo?" and defend it.
4. **Write** a one-paragraph position.
