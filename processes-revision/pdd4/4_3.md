# 4.3 | Black & White, Tonal Range & the Histogram

{: .note }
**Existing lesson, lightly edited.** From the current PDD course: EX20 Ansel Adams. Draft revision page; the original files are untouched.

Ansel Adams made some of the most famous photographs ever, without any color. Today you'll learn how tone does the work.

## Bell Ringer / Hook (~10 min)

{: .discussion }
What does a black-and-white photo take away? What does it leave?

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

A **histogram** graphs how many pixels sit at each brightness, from black on the left to white on the right. A photo that touches both ends has full **tonal range**. **Color mode** (RGB, CMYK, Grayscale) and **bit depth** (8 or 16 bits per channel) set how many tones are possible. Convert to B&W with a **Black & White adjustment layer**, not by changing the mode, so it stays editable, then fix tones with **Levels**. Save settings you like as an **adjustment preset**.

> "This histogram is bunched in the middle: no true blacks, no true whites. That's why it looks flat. Watch me drag the Levels sliders in to the edges."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What does the left side of a histogram show?"

> **Analysis:** "Why use an adjustment layer instead of Image → Mode → Grayscale?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "How would you use the B&W color sliders to make a red jersey pop against green grass?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Open** a photo with a range of colors.
2. **Add a Black & White adjustment layer** and move the color sliders.
3. **Add Levels** and stretch the tones to the histogram's edges.
4. **Save a preset** from the adjustment's menu; Turn and Talk about your settings.

{: .discussion }
When does a photo work better in black and white than in color?
