# 4.2 | Shaping Light: Studio Patterns & Off-Camera Flash

Studio photographers don't hope for good light; they build it. Today you'll build five classic portrait looks.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

Portrait lighting patterns are named for the shadow they make. **Split**: half the face lit. **Loop**: a small nose shadow loops toward the cheek. **Rembrandt**: a triangle of light on the shadow-side cheek. **Butterfly**: a shadow under the nose from light high in front. **Broad/short**: lighting the side of the face toward or away from the camera. The **main** light makes the pattern; a **fill** softens the shadows. The **lighting ratio** compares them in stops.

> "Main reads f/8, fill reads f/5.6. That's one stop apart, a 2:1 ratio. Gentle shadows."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "Which pattern makes a triangle of light on the cheek?"

> **Analysis:** "Why would a photographer choose split light for a dramatic sports portrait?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Your subject has glasses and the flash reflects. What would you change, and why?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Set up** the trigger and flash with the guide (clear settings, M mode, channel and group).
2. **Station rotation:** light and shoot split, loop, and Rembrandt.
3. **Meter** the main and fill at one station and record the ratio.
4. **Turn and Talk:** which pattern suits which kind of portrait?

{: .discussion }
How does moving the main light change the subject's mood?
