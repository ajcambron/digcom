# 4.1 | Quality of Light

{: .note }
**Existing lesson, lightly edited.** From the current PDD course: EX17 Lighting. Draft revision page; the original files are untouched.

Photography means 'drawing with light.' Today you'll learn to describe it, and then to choose it.

## Bell Ringer / Hook (~10 min)

{: .discussion }
Why do photos taken at noon often look harsh, and photos at sunset look warm?

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

Light has a **quality**: **hard** light (small source, sharp shadows) or **soft** light (large or diffused source, gentle shadows). It has a **direction**: front, side, or back. It has a **color**, measured in **Kelvin**: candlelight about 1900 K, daylight about 5500 K, shade about 7000 K. **White balance** tells the camera what color 'white' should be.

> "I'm going to move this lamp from the front to the side. Watch what happens to the texture on the face."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "Which makes softer light: a bare bulb or a window with a curtain?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does side light show texture better than front light?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "You're shooting under orange gym lights. What white balance do you choose, and what happens if you leave it on Auto?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Direction set:** photograph your partner lit from the front, side, and back.
2. **White balance set:** shoot the same frame on Daylight, Tungsten, and Auto.
3. **Fill in the chart** for all six frames.
4. **Turn and Talk:** which direction flattered your subject most?

{: .discussion }
What mood does side light create compared to front light?
