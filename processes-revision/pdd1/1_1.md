# 1.1 | Intro to Digital Photography

{: .note }
**Existing lesson, reordered, content unchanged.** From the current PDD course: EX02 Intro to Digital Photography (edex + Photography Basics). Draft revision page; the original files are untouched.

This year you'll learn to make photographs on purpose instead of by accident. It starts with knowing your camera.

## Bell Ringer / Hook (~10 min)

{: .discussion }
What's one photo you've seen this week that stopped your scroll? What made it work?

## Direct Instruction (~12 min)

A camera is a dark box that lets light in for a controlled moment. The **lens** focuses the light, the **shutter** decides how long it comes in, and the **sensor** records it as millions of **pixels**. The more pixels, the higher the **resolution**. Today you'll learn where each of those lives on our cameras.

> "Every photo you've ever taken was a decision about light. This year you make that decision instead of the phone."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "Which part of the camera records the light?"

> **Analysis:** "Why might a photographer choose a DSLR over a phone for a sports game?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If a sensor has more pixels but is the same size, what might that change about the photo?"

## Guided Practice & Shoot (~50 min)

1. **Camera parts hunt:** with your partner, find every part on the diagram on the actual camera body. Point and name it out loud.
2. **Turn and Talk:** which three parts do you think you'll touch most? Why?
3. **Practice frames:** set the mode dial to Auto and take 10 frames around the room: 3 close, 3 medium, 3 wide, and 1 of your choice.
4. **Check your card:** review the frames on the LCD screen. Star your best one.

{: .discussion }
What surprised you about how many controls the camera has? Which one do you most want to learn first?

{: .important }
**Competition track (optional enrichment):** Start a SkillsUSA written-test flashcard deck: one card per camera part, term on the front, job on the back.

## Exit Ticket / Wrap-Up (~6 min)

{: .reflection }
Which part of the camera did you understand least today, and what's your question about it?
