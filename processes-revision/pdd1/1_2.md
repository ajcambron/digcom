## Direct Instruction (~12 min)

Exposure is how much light reaches the sensor. Three settings control it. **Aperture** (f-number) is the size of the opening; a small f-number like f/4 is a big opening. **Shutter speed** is how long it stays open; 1/1000 freezes action, 1/15 blurs it. **ISO** is how sensitive the sensor is; higher ISO is brighter but grainier. Each full step, called a **stop**, doubles or halves the light.

> "Watch the light meter in the viewfinder. When the needle sits on zero, the camera thinks this exposure is correct. Now I can get to zero three different ways."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "Which f-number lets in more light: f/4 or f/11?"

> **Analysis:** "Why would a sports photographer raise ISO instead of slowing the shutter?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "If you need both a fast shutter and lots of depth of field in a dim gym, what has to give, and why?"

## Guided Practice & Shoot (~50 min)

1. **CameraSim warm-up:** complete the handout's three challenges (freeze the windmill, blur the windmill, keep the background sharp).
2. **Switch to M:** set ISO 400 and center the light meter on one subject using aperture and shutter.
3. **Three ways to zero:** make two more correct exposures of the same subject with different combinations. Record each in your settings log.
4. **Think-Pair-Share:** compare your three frames with your partner. Which looks different, even though they're all 'correct'?

{: .discussion }
Why can two photos with the same exposure look completely different?
