## Direct Instruction (~12 min)

**Rule of thirds** places the subject on the grid lines, not dead center. **Leading lines** pull the eye toward the subject. **Framing** surrounds the subject with something in the scene (a doorway, branches). **Viewpoint** changes the story: shoot low to make a subject powerful, high to make it small. **Negative space** gives the subject room. **Aspect ratio** is the shape of the frame: our cameras shoot 3:2; Instagram prefers 4:5.

> "Turn on the grid in Live View. I put her eye on this intersection, not in the middle, and now the photo has somewhere to go."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What is the rule of thirds?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does a low viewpoint make a subject look more powerful?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Pick one technique you'd use to make a lonely-looking photo. Explain your choice."

## Guided Practice & Shoot (~50 min)

1. **Turn on the grid** in Live View (Menu → Grid display).
2. **Scavenger hunt:** shoot one frame for each technique: rule of thirds, leading lines, framing, low viewpoint, high viewpoint, negative space.
3. **Gallery walk:** show your six frames to another pair. They guess the technique before you tell them.
4. **Pick your best** and write one sentence on why it works.

{: .discussion }
Which technique made the biggest difference to your photos today?
