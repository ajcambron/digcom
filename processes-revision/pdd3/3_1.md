## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

Every assignment starts with **purpose** (what's the story?) and **audience** (who will see it?). A **shot list** makes sure you come back with a full story: a **wide** establishing shot, **medium** action, a **close-up**, a **detail**, and a **reaction**. **Framing** within the scene isolates the subject. A strong news photo shows emotion, action, and a decisive moment.

> "If my editor can only run one of these, it's the one with a face reacting. Emotion beats everything."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "Name the five shot types on a shot list."

> **Analysis:** "Why does a reaction shot often tell more of the story than the action?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Write the one photo you'd need to tell this event's story if you could only take one."

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Think-Pair-Share:** for each of three news photos, name the story, the shot type, and the framing.
2. **Choose your event** and write its purpose and audience in one sentence each.
3. **Write a shot list** of at least eight shots, with when each is likely to happen.
4. **Trade lists** with a partner. They mark one shot that's missing.

{: .discussion }
What makes a photo 'news' instead of just a nice picture?
