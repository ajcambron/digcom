# 3.3 | Photo Usage Rights & Releases

{: .note }
**Existing lesson, lightly edited.** From the current PDD course: EX13 Photo Usage Rights + Photo Search Skills. Draft revision page; the original files are untouched.

Just because you can find an image doesn't mean you can use it. And photographing people comes with responsibilities.

{% include lesson-parts/stinger.md %}

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

**Copyright** belongs to whoever made the image, automatically. You can use someone else's work if it's **public domain**, if the **license** allows it (Creative Commons licenses say how: attribution, no commercial use, no changes), or under limited **fair use**. AI-generated images raise their own rules. If a person is recognizable in a photo used to promote something, you need a **model release**; private property may need a **location release**.

> "I'll read this license out loud. CC BY-NC: I must credit the photographer, and I can't use it to sell anything."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "Who owns the copyright to a photo you take?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does a recognizable face in an advertisement need a release when a news photo usually doesn't?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "You want to use a classmate's photo in your TSA portfolio. What do you need, and from whom?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Sort the scenarios** with your partner: can use / can use with conditions / can't use.
2. **Cold call:** defend one sort with the license or rule.
3. **Fill out a model release** for your partner as practice.
4. **Read** the TSA Student Copyright Checklist and highlight the parts that apply to photos.

{: .discussion }
Is it fair to use a photo you found online if you give credit? Why or why not?
