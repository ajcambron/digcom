# 3.2 | Cropping, Resolution & Image Size

{: .note }

A crop can make or break a photo, and a resize done wrong ruins it. Today you'll do both right.

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

**Crop** to remove distractions and strengthen composition. The Crop tool can lock an **aspect ratio** (4:5, 16:9) and **Straighten** a tilted horizon. **Resolution** (ppi) only matters for print: pixels ÷ ppi = inches. In **Image → Image Size**, unchecking **Resample** changes ppi without changing pixels; checking it adds or throws away pixels.

> "Watch the pixel count. If I uncheck Resample, it doesn't move. I'm not changing the photo, just how big it prints."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What does ppi stand for?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does making an image bigger with Resample on often look soft?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "A client needs this photo for a 24 × 36 in poster. Can it work? Show the math."

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Crop three ways:** tight, rule-of-thirds, and a 4:5 social crop. Save each.
2. **Straighten** one tilted photo with the Crop tool's Straighten option.
3. **Resize for print:** 8 × 10 in at 300 ppi. Note the pixel dimensions.
4. **Resize for screen:** 1080 px wide. Turn and Talk: which resize lost information?

{: .discussion }
When is it okay to crop a news photo, and when would cropping change the truth?
