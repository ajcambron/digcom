# 3.4 | Captions & Cutlines

{: .note }
**Existing lesson, lightly edited.** From the current PDD course: EX10 Craft the Cutline. Draft revision page; the original files are untouched.

A photo without a caption leaves readers guessing. Today you'll write captions that tell the whole story.

## Bell Ringer / Hook (~10 min)

{: .discussion }
Read this caption: 'People at a game.' What's missing?

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

A **cutline** answers **who, what, when, where, why** in present tense, with full names and identifiers. A **TSA image statement** is longer: camera make and model, **f-stop**, **exposure time**, **ISO**, **focal length**, a description, how it fits the theme, and the edits you made.

> "'Senior Maya Ortiz celebrates…' Present tense, full name, grade. The reader knows exactly who and what."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What are the 5 Ws?"

> **Analysis:** "Why are cutlines written in present tense?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "How could the same photo need a different cutline for the yearbook and for a contest statement?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Fix three weak cutlines** from the deck with your partner.
2. **Pick one of your photos** and write its cutline.
3. **Write its TSA statement** using your settings log for the metadata.
4. **Fact-check swap:** your partner circles anything they can't verify.

{: .discussion }
Why do you think TSA wants the camera settings in the statement?
