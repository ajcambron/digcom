# 6.4 | Typographic Design

{: .note }
**Existing lesson, lightly edited.** From the current PDD course: EX30 Typographic Design 1 & 2. Draft revision page; the original files are untouched.

Type tells the reader where to look. Today you'll control it.

## Bell Ringer / Hook (~10 min)

{: .discussion }
Look at a magazine cover. What do you read first, second, third? Why?

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

**Hierarchy** is the order the eye reads: size, weight, color, and position decide it. **Point type** is for short lines; **paragraph type** flows in a box. The **Character** panel controls font, size, **kerning**, **tracking**, and **leading**; the **Paragraph** panel controls alignment and spacing. Type can follow a path or be warped. Keep it live; **rasterize** only when you must.

> "Headline 72 pt, deck 24 pt, body 11 pt. The size jumps do the work."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's the difference between kerning and tracking?"

> **Analysis:** "Why keep type live instead of rasterizing?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Design the hierarchy for an event poster with five pieces of information."

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Complete** the Lesson07 project.
2. **Add Type on a Path** to one element.
3. **Run Check Spelling.**
4. **Turn and Talk:** rank your partner's hierarchy.

{: .discussion }
What makes a type choice feel right for a photo?
