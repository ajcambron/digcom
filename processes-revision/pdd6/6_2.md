# 6.2 | Double Exposure

{: .note }
**Existing lesson, reordered, content unchanged.** From the current PDD course: EX16 Double Exposure. Draft revision page; the original files are untouched.

A double exposure puts two photos in one frame so they tell one story.

## Bell Ringer / Hook (~10 min)

{: .discussion }
Look at this double exposure. Which two images do you see, and how do they relate?

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

Select the subject and mask it. Place the second image above it and **clip** it (Option-click between layers) so it shows only inside the subject. Change the **blend mode** to Screen or Lighten so the portrait's shadows let the landscape through. **Paste Into** (Edit → Paste Special) is another way to put an image inside a selection.

> "Option-click the line between layers. The forest only exists inside her silhouette now."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What does a clipping mask do?"

> **Analysis:** "Why does Screen blend mode work well here?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "Which landscape would tell the strongest story with your portrait?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Mask your portrait** with Select Subject.
2. **Clip a landscape** to it.
3. **Try three blend modes** and keep the best.
4. **Gallery walk** with a partner.

{: .discussion }
What makes a double exposure meaningful instead of just interesting?

## GMetrix/BrainBuffet (~30 min)
