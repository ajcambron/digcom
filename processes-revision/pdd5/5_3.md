# 5.3 | Blur & Depth of Field

{: .note }
**Existing lesson, lightly edited.** From the current PDD course: EX22 Blur, EX25 Depth of Field. Draft revision page; the original files are untouched.

Blur directs the eye. Today you'll add it in Photoshop, in a way you can change later.

## Bell Ringer / Hook (~10 min)

{: .discussion }
Can you fake a shallow depth of field in Photoshop? Should you, if you could have done it in camera?

## Direct Instruction (~10 min)

A **destructive** edit changes pixels permanently. A **nondestructive** edit can be changed or removed. Convert a layer to a **Smart Object**, and every filter becomes a **Smart Filter** you can reopen, with a mask to control where it applies. The **Blur Gallery** has Field, Iris, and Tilt-Shift blurs.

> "Double-click the filter name. I can still change the blur amount, even after saving and closing."

**Scaffolded questions. Cold-call through all three:**

> **Recall:** "What's a Smart Filter?"

> **Analysis:** "Why is nondestructive editing safer for a client project?"

> **Synthesis/Extension:** "When should you blur in camera instead of in Photoshop?"

## Guided Practice (~23 min)

1. **Convert** the photo to a Smart Object.
2. **Add Iris Blur** to fake shallow depth of field; mask the subject.
3. **Add Path or Motion Blur** to a second image.
4. **Swap files** with your partner and change their blur amounts.

{: .discussion }
Which is more honest: blur in camera or blur in Photoshop?

## GMetrix/BrainBuffet (~30 min)

Log in and keep working in your BrainBuffet module: BrainBuffet Photoshop Module 2 (Retouch or Real), ~2 h. Start wherever you left off.
